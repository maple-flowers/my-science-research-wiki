> ### 1. 研究背景与问题
> 
> **铁磁性**（**Ferromagnetism**）是一种独特的磁性现象，指某些材料在没有外部磁场的情况下也能表现出**自发磁化**（spontaneous magnetization），且这种磁化能在特定温度（**居里点**，**Curie point**）以下维持。早期的现象学描述取得了显著成功，其中最突出的是**魏斯分子场**（**Weiss Molecular Field**）理论（1907年）。
> 
> **魏斯分子场理论**的核心思想是，铁磁材料中的每个原子磁矩除了受到外部磁场**H**的作用外，还受到一个内部“分子场”**qM**的作用，其中**M**是材料的宏观磁化强度，**q**是一个与温度无关的比例因子。通过将**朗之万函数**（**Langevin function**）的参数从**H**替换为**H+qM**，魏斯理论能够成功地解释铁磁材料的居里点以及饱和磁化强度随温度的变化，其预测的**对应态定律**（Law of Corresponding States）在归一化单位下显示出普遍性（如图3中的经典曲线S=∞）。
> 
> 然而，魏斯理论本质上是现象学的，未能从微观层面解释“分子场”**qM**的起源。当时的物理学家尝试用已知的**经典偶极-偶极相互作用**（classical dipole-dipole coupling）来解释，但发现其强度远不足以产生所需的巨大分子场（所需**q**值远大于经典预测）。此外，经典偶极相互作用预测磁晶各向异性（magnetic anisotropy）应是“一级效应”，即磁化方向强烈依赖于晶体切割方向，这与实验观察到的各向异性通常是“二级效应”（饱和磁化强度在各方向大致相同，但达到饱和所需的磁场不同）不符，并且预测的居里点温度过低，与实际的几百开尔文（10³ °K）相去甚远。这些**经典理论的缺陷**构成了铁磁性研究的核心问题，亟待新的物理机制来解释。
> 
> ### 2. 方法模型与理论
> 
> #### 2.1 量子力学的到来与交换力的引入
> 
> 1926年**量子力学**的诞生，尤其是**电子自旋**（**electron spin**）概念的引入，为解决铁磁性之谜带来了曙光。
> 
> -   **电子自旋与旋磁比**：**乌伦贝克-高德斯密特**（Uhlenbeck-Goudsmit）的电子自旋概念指出，电子的磁矩与角动量之比（**旋磁比**，**gyromagnetic ratio**）为e/mc，而非经典理论的e/2mc。这与实验中观察到的铁磁材料旋磁比接近2的事实相符（排除硫铁矿等少数例外），表明**轨道角动量猝灭**（**quenching of orbital angular momentum**）在固态中是普遍现象，磁性主要由自旋贡献。
> -   **布洛赫函数**：由于自旋是量子化的，其取向是离散的，因此在量子理论中，**布洛赫函数**（**Brillouin function**）替代了经典的朗之万函数来描述磁化强度与磁场、温度的关系。
> -   **交换力解释分子场**：量子力学对铁磁性最根本的贡献是**海森堡**（Heisenberg, 1928年）提出的**交换力**（**exchange forces**）概念。这些力源于轨道波函数的重叠和**泡利不相容原理**（Pauli exclusion principle）的约束，导致轨道对称性与自旋排列之间存在关联，从而产生巨大的“视在自旋-自旋耦合”。这种力虽然本质上与电子的磁矩无关，但却能解释魏斯分子场所需的巨大作用强度。**狄拉克**（Dirac）表明，交换力可等效为一个**交换积分**（**exchange integral**）`J_ij`描述的势能`U_ij = -2J_ij S_i ⋅ S_j`，其中`S_i`和`S_j`是原子i和j的自旋角动量矢量。交换积分的特性是：
>     -   其值可以很大，从而解释了魏斯分子场所需的强大相互作用。
>     -   势能是**各向同性的**（isotropic），不依赖于自旋磁矩与原子间连线的相对取向，这与实验中观察到的铁磁各向异性是二级效应（而非经典理论预测的一级效应）相符。虽然存在一些各向异性，但主要归因于较小的经典电磁耦合和**自旋-轨道耦合**（spin-orbit coupling）的不完全猝灭。
> 
> #### 2.2 海森堡模型的近似与挑战
> 
> 尽管交换力提供了微观基础，但基于海森堡模型进行定量计算极为复杂，需要借助各种近似方法。
> 
> -   **斯托纳的简单方法**：**斯托纳**（Stoner）提出了一种简化计算方法，即将晶体中其他自旋对给定自旋的瞬时作用替换为其时间平均值。这种近似下，魏斯分子场常数**q**可表示为`q = zJ/(2Sμ_B)^2`，其中**z**是最近邻原子数，**μ_B**是玻尔磁子。由此可导出**居里温度**`T_c = 2zJS(S+1)/(3k)`（Eq. 15）。这种方法在一定程度上将魏斯理论的现象学参数与微观的交换积分联系起来。
> -   **高级近似与收敛性问题**：海森堡本人和**奥佩霍夫斯基**（Opechowski）尝试通过**磁化率倒数**（1/χ）在居里点以上对温度**T**进行级数展开（Eq. 16）来引入更高级的近似，以考虑分子场的波动。然而，这些尝试遭遇了严重的**收敛性问题**（poor convergence），导致结果不尽人意。例如，第二近似（高斯假设）预测某些晶格（如简单立方）根本不会出现居里点，或对铁磁性条件过于苛刻；而第一近似则过于宽松。这表明简单地增加级数项的复杂性并不总能提高理论的准确性。
> -   **费伊的半严格方法**：**费伊**（Fay）提出将晶体分解为基本立方体，在立方体内部严格考虑交换相互作用，而立方体之间则采用第一近似方法。他发现这种“半严格”处理与完全使用第一近似的结果差异不大，未能显著改进魏斯理论的简单公式。
> -   **伊辛模型**：**伊辛模型**（**Ising model**）是对海森堡模型的一种简化，它只考虑自旋在磁场方向上的分量`s_i^z`之间的相互作用（`-2Js_i^z s_j^z`），而忽略了垂直分量。虽然伊辛模型在数学上更易处理（尤其对一维或二维晶格），但在物理上被认为是“纯粹的数学虚构”，因为它忽略了重要的物理相互作用，其结果不应与真实材料行为过于紧密地对应。
> 
> #### 2.3 贝特-皮尔斯-魏斯方法
> 
> **彼得·魏斯**（P. R. Weiss, 与P. Weiss不同）将**贝特-皮尔斯**（Bethe-Peierls）在合金有序-无序问题中的方法应用于铁磁性。该方法的核心思想是将一个中心原子及其**z**个最近邻原子视为一个结构单元，严格处理单元内部的相互作用，而将单元外部原子的影响通过一个**有效场**（effective field H_e）来近似。这种方法有以下特点：
> 
> -   **更通用的有效场**：**H_e**比魏斯理论中的**qM**更通用，它不一定与**M**呈线性关系，且系数可以依赖于温度。
> -   **闭合形式解**：与级数展开不同，贝特-皮尔斯-魏斯方法能够得到**闭合形式**（closed expressions）的解，避免了收敛性问题。
> -   **敏感度高**：该方法能够区分不同晶格结构（如简单立方与平面六角晶格），预测前者具有居里点而后者不具有，这与更严格的理论（如布洛赫自旋波）结果一致。
> -   **改进的近似**：彼得·魏斯的计算结果介于海森堡模型的第一近似和第二近似之间，比高斯假设更接近实验。然而，该方法在居里点以下（铁磁区域）的计算更为困难，且在**低温度**下可能不准确，因为此时**长程有序**（long-range order）和**自旋波**（spin waves）效应变得重要，而贝特-皮尔斯模型未能完全涵盖。
> 
> #### 2.4 布洛赫自旋波理论
> 
> **布洛赫**（Bloch）开发的**自旋波**（**spin waves**）理论特别适用于**低温度区域**（远低于居里点）。其基本假设是，当只有少数自旋反转时（接近完全饱和），这些反转的自旋可以在晶体中以波的形式传播，形成“自旋波”。
> 
> -   **适用范围**：该方法仅适用于**接近完全饱和**（M≈Np）和**极低温度**（kT/J很小）的情况，因为其近似建立在反转自旋数量很少的假设上。
> -   **磁化强度与温度关系**：布洛赫理论预测在低温度下，磁化强度随温度的变化遵循`M = Nμ_B (1 - A(kT/J)^(3/2))`（Eq. 20）的形式，其中**A**为数值常数。这种`T^(3/2)`的依赖关系被实验证实，表明在低温下磁化强度比魏斯分子场理论预测的更快地偏离饱和值。
> -   **晶格结构区分**：布洛赫理论能够区分平面六角晶格和立方晶格，预测前者即使在绝对零度也不会是铁磁性的，而后者会是，这与彼得·魏斯方法的结论一致。
> 
> #### 2.5 斯托纳巡游电子模型
> 
> 与海森堡的**海特勒-伦敦模型**（**Heitler-London model**）所代表的**非极性近似**（non-polar approximation，即电子局域在原子上不参与导电）相对，**斯托纳**（Stoner）发展了一种基于**巡游电子**（**itinerant electrons**）的铁磁性理论。该模型认为3d电子在晶体中自由移动，其能量级别是整个晶体的属性，并遵循**费米-狄拉克统计**（**Fermi-Dirac statistics**）。
> 
> -   **核心思想**：斯托纳方法将**魏斯分子场**叠加到**索末菲**（Sommerfeld）的电子传导理论上，通过**特征函数**（characteristic function, Eq. 21）来计算自由能、比热和磁矩。
> -   **关键参数**：引入了无量纲参数**kθ'/ε0**，表示**交换积分**与**费米能量**（Fermi energy）展宽的比率。
> -   **主要预测与成功**：
>     1.  **非整数玻尔磁子数**：斯托纳理论预测在绝对零度下的**饱和磁化强度**（saturation intensity, l0）不一定是整数个**玻尔磁子**（Bohr magnetons），这与实验观察相符（如图5所示）。
>     2.  **额外比热**：理论预测存在一个与温度呈**线性关系**（linear in T）的电子**比热**（specific heat），被称为“额外比热”，这也被实验证实，是镍等过渡元素的特征。
>     3.  **磁化率倒数曲线**：在居里点以上，磁化率倒数近似呈线性，但仍有明显曲率，特别是接近居里点时。斜率与魏斯-海森堡理论的第一项预测值不同。
> -   **局限性**：斯托纳理论未能完全或严格地处理交换相互作用，并且**忽略了电子关联能**（**correlation energy**），即电子之间瞬时排斥力造成的相互作用。这可能导致对特定原子上电荷盈余或亏缺的过度估计，从而可能与真实情况存在偏差。
> 
> #### 2.6 交换积分的符号问题
> 
> 为了实现铁磁性，**交换积分**`J`必须为**正值**（positive）。如果`J`为负，则交换效应会使自旋趋于反平行排列，表现出退磁效应或反铁磁性。虽然从理论上计算`J`的符号和大小非常困难，但**斯莱特**（Slater）提出了一个定性经验法则：当**原子半径**（atomic radius）远小于**原子间距**（interatomic distance）时，更可能出现铁磁性。
> 
> **索末菲**（Sommerfeld）和**贝特**（Bethe）对斯莱特法则提供了解释：交换积分的贡献主要来自波函数最外层节点之外的区域。当原子半径小、原子间距大时，波函数重叠主要发生在两原子中间的“空隙”区域，此时电子间的排斥项可能压过原子核的吸引项，导致**交换积分**为正。此外，**d电子**和**f电子**由于其波函数中的`r^l`因子（l为角量子数）在原子内部（核力最强处）贡献较小，使得外部区域的排斥效应更容易占主导，从而有利于形成正的交换积分，解释了为何铁磁性主要出现在d和f电子体系中，而s电子则否。
> 
> #### 2.7 弱顺磁性与反铁磁性
> 
> -   **弱顺磁性**：当**交换积分**为**负值**时，交换效应会产生退磁作用，导致自旋倾向于反平行排列。这通常对应于**弱顺磁性**（**feeble paramagnetism**），其磁化率较低且与温度关系不大（如铝）。此外，**泡利**（Pauli）指出，电子在固体导体中迁移导致的能带展宽也能显著抑制顺磁性。对于s或p电子，由于其波函数重叠广泛，迁移效应通常足以抑制大部分顺磁性。
> -   **反铁磁性**：是一种特殊的负交换积分导致的磁性。在**反铁磁材料**（**anti-ferromagnetic materials**）中（如MnO），晶体可以被分为两个子晶格A和B，其中A子晶格的自旋指向一个方向，B子晶格的自旋指向相反方向，从而导致宏观上总磁矩为零或很小。这种材料的磁化率在一个特定温度（**奈尔温度**，Néel temperature，对应于图6中的T_c）达到**最大值**，然后随温度降低而减小，这与弱顺磁材料的单调下降或温度无关行为不同。**奈尔**（Néel）和**比特定**（Bitter）的理论解释了这种现象。在绝对零度，如果外部磁场垂直于交错的内部场，磁化率是有限的，约为奈尔温度处磁化率的1/3。这表明即使在强场下，反铁磁材料的磁化强度也远小于铁磁材料。
> 
> ### 3. 比较与讨论
> 
> 文章对海森堡（束缚电子）和斯托纳（巡游电子）两种主流理论进行了深入的**比较**。两者在许多方面表现出相似之处，例如都能合理解释居里点以下饱和磁化强度随温度的变化（图3和图4的左侧部分）。然而，在细节上存在差异：**布洛赫自旋波理论**（Heisenberg模型的一种变体）在解释绝对零度下磁化强度随温度的趋近规律（`T^(3/2)`）方面比斯托纳理论（`T^2`）与实验符合得更好。
> 
> **斯托纳理论**的独特优势在于能够解释**非整数玻尔磁子数**（item 1）和与温度呈线性关系的**额外比热**（item 6）。尽管文章指出，通过对**海森堡模型**稍作修改（例如，镍原子配置为3d⁹和3d¹⁰的混合态），也能在定性上解释这些现象，但这需要引入额外的假设和参数。
> 
> 一个核心的**争论点**是**电子关联能**（correlation energy）。斯托纳理论在处理巡游电子时**忽略了关联能**，这意味着它可能高估了特定原子上电荷的盈余或亏缺。而**维格纳**（Wigner）和**塞茨**（Seitz）认为关联效应是相当大的。文章倾向于认为，对海森堡-海特勒-伦敦模型的泛化（如3d⁹和3d¹⁰构型混合）在一定程度上是**束缚电子**和**巡游电子**模型之间的一种**妥协**，可能更接近真实情况。因为这种泛化不涉及巡游电子模型中那种高度的瞬时极性。
> 
> 文章也承认，要断言哪种理论“最接近现实”是困难的，因为它们都只是极限情况的近似，真实情况可能介于两者之间。最终，通过加入适当的修正项（如斯托纳模型中的高电离态，或海森堡模型中的关联效应），两者可能会趋于融合。
> 
> **定量预测的普遍挑战**：所有理论在进行精确定量预测时都面临巨大挑战，特别是对于**剩磁**（remanence）、**磁滞**（hysteresis）等复杂现象。文章特别指出，实验中观察到的**磁化率倒数**1/χ与温度**T**在居里点以上近似**线性**的关系，在理论上是一个“谜团”。因为各种理论模型（如海森堡的级数展开）预测会存在更多曲率，且级数收敛性很差，导致简单的前几项近似无法充分解释这种线性。这暗示了可能存在多种复杂效应（分子场波动、自旋-轨道耦合残留、电子迁移、构型再分配等）的意外补偿，使得总体行为近似线性。
> 
> **钆（Gadolinium）的特例**：**特龙布**（Trombe）对钆的实验研究提供了一个有趣的案例。由于钆的4f电子深埋在原子内部，且Gd+++离子处于S态，因此**自旋-轨道耦合**和**电子迁移**效应可以忽略。在这种情况下，简单的**海森堡理论第一近似**（S=7/2）竟然能够“异常良好地”解释1/χ与T的线性关系，且饱和磁化强度在绝对零度与理论值高度一致。这表明当某些复杂因素被最小化时，简单的理论模型能够提供深刻的物理图像。但文章也指出，即使在这种情况下，分子场的波动效应（级数更高项）也应该存在，为何其影响显得不重要，仍是一个未解之谜。这可能与钆原子较高的自旋S值有关，高自旋可能会减小波动。
> 
> ## 五、结论与贡献
> 
> ### 1. 核心发现
> 
> 这篇1945年的综述文章对铁磁性理论的发展史和当时的研究现状进行了深刻总结，其核心发现包括：
> 
> -   **量子力学的胜利**：**交换力**的发现是铁磁性理论的里程碑。它成功地为**魏斯分子场**提供了微观量子起源，解决了经典理论无法解释其巨大强度和各向同性这一核心难题。
> -   **多模型并存与互补**：海森堡模型（束缚电子）、斯托纳模型（巡游电子）、布洛赫自旋波理论和贝特-皮尔斯-魏斯方法等多种理论模型，各自在特定方面或温度区域对铁磁现象提供了合理的解释。虽然没有一个完美无缺的理论能够完全定量地解释所有现象，但它们共同构成了理解铁磁性的多元视角。
> -   **交换积分的关键作用**：**交换积分**的符号是决定材料磁性的关键。正的交换积分是形成铁磁性的必要条件，而负的交换积分则导致**弱顺磁性**或**反铁磁性**。
> -   **铁磁性的特殊性**：铁磁性可以被视为对通常**化学键理论**（其中自旋倾向于反平行配对以形成饱和键）的一种例外，因为它要求自旋平行排列以达到最低能量状态。
> -   **理论与实验的差距**：尽管量子理论取得了巨大成功，但在精确**定量预测**如剩磁、磁滞、以及解释**磁化率倒数与温度的近似线性关系**（一个被作者称为“谜团”的现象）方面，仍存在显著不足。这反映了多体量子力学在处理真实复杂固态系统时的内在挑战。
> 
> ### 2. 理论/实践贡献
> 
> J. H. VAN VLECK的这篇综述文章具有重要的理论和历史**贡献**：
> 
> -   **历史性里程碑**：它清晰地勾勒了铁磁性理论从经典现象学向量子力学解释的范式转变，为后来的研究奠定了坚实的基础，是早期量子力学应用于固态物理领域的经典文献之一。
> -   **批判性评估框架**：文章不仅介绍理论，更重要的是对其进行了深入的**批判性分析**，指出了各个模型的适用范围、近似的优点和局限性、以及数学上的收敛性问题。这种批判性思维对于引导后续研究至关重要。
> -   **模型融合的启示**：通过比较束缚电子（海森堡）和巡游电子（斯托纳）两种极限模型，文章启发了研究者去探索真实材料中处于两者之间的**混合模型**，并认识到**电子关联效应**的重要性。
> -   **类比与统一**：文章将铁磁性理论与**化学键理论**进行了类比，强调了在处理复杂多电子系统时，两种看似不同的物理现象可能共享共同的量子力学原理和挑战。这有助于构建更统一的物理学图景。
> -   **指明未来方向**：文章明确指出了当时未解决的问题和理论的不足，实际上为战后凝聚态物理学在铁磁性领域的研究指明了方向，例如对更精确计算方法、电子关联效应和复杂现象（如滞后）的深入探索。
> 
> ## 六、未来发展方向展望
> 
> 基于1945年这篇文献的视角，我们可以总结出当时存在的**未解决的问题**以及由此衍生的**潜在研究路径**，其中许多在随后的几十年里成为了凝聚态物理学的研究热点。
> 
> ### 1. 未解决的问题
> 
> -   **精确计算的挑战**：海森堡模型及其高级近似普遍存在**收敛性差**的问题，无法提供对磁化率、居里点等性质的精确定量预测。如何发展更准确、更具系统性的计算方法是一个核心挑战。
> -   **微观机制的融合**：海森堡模型（束缚电子，非极性）和斯托纳模型（巡游电子，极性）代表了两个极端。真实材料中电子行为可能介于两者之间。如何构建一个能统一或融合这两种极限情况的理论，准确描述**电子关联效应**，是亟待解决的问题。斯托纳模型对关联能的忽略是一个明显的不足。
> -   **复杂现象的解释**：诸如**剩磁**、**磁滞**和**矫顽力**（coercivity）等重要的铁磁性宏观现象，当时的所有理论都未能给出令人满意的微观解释。
> -   **磁化率-温度曲线的线性之谜**：实验中观察到的居里点以上1/χ对T的近似线性关系，在理论上仍是一个“谜团”。未能解释其背后的**偶然补偿机制**（fortuitous compensation）以及各种复杂因素（分子场波动、自旋-轨道耦合、电子迁移、构型重分布）的精确作用。
> -   **交换积分的理论计算**：精确计算铁磁材料中**交换积分**的符号和大小仍然极具挑战，限制了理论对特定材料是否具有铁磁性的**预测能力**。
> -   **低温行为的更深层次理解**：尽管布洛赫自旋波理论成功解释了极低温度下的行为，但其假设（少数反转自旋）限制了其适用范围。在更广阔的低温区域（但非极低），长程有序和自旋波之外的效应仍需深入研究。
> -   **自旋-轨道耦合的作用**：虽然文章提到自旋-轨道耦合影响了各向异性，但其具体作用机制和定量计算在当时尚未被详细考虑。
> 
> ### 2. 潜在研究路径
> 
> -   **发展更高级的量子多体理论和计算方法**：
>     -   探索超越平均场和简单级数展开的更精确的近似方法，例如**格林函数方法**、**多体微扰理论**等，以更准确地处理电子间的相互作用。
>     -   利用新兴的**计算物理**和**量子化学**技术（如第一性原理计算，基于密度泛函理论DFT），从头计算**交换积分**和能带结构，预测材料的磁性，而非依赖于定性经验法则。
> -   **构建统一的电子磁性理论**：
>     -   发展能够桥接**局域电子**和**巡游电子**模型之间鸿沟的理论，例如将电子关联效应（Hubbard模型、DMFT等）更严格地纳入斯托纳类模型中，或将**电荷涨落**（charge fluctuations）和**构型混合**（configurational mixing）更系统地引入海森堡模型。
> -   **深入研究复杂磁现象的微观机制**：
>     -   通过对**自旋动力学**（spin dynamics）和**磁畴**（magnetic domain）演化的理论建模，来解释剩磁、磁滞和矫顽力等宏观现象的微观起源。这需要考虑**缺陷**、**杂质**和**晶格应变**等对磁畴结构和运动的影响。
> -   **探索新型铁磁材料和体系**：
>     -   结合材料科学的发展，研究具有特定晶体结构、低维特性（如薄膜、纳米结构）或特殊电子构型的材料，以检验现有理论的适用性，并发现新的磁性物理现象。
> -   **利用新型实验技术验证理论**：
>     -   发展更高分辨率、更精确的磁性测量技术（如中子散射、同步辐射），获取原子尺度的磁结构和自旋动力学信息，为理论模型提供更严格的检验。
> -   **考虑有限温度下的自旋涨落**：
>     -   在居里点附近的温度区域，**自旋涨落**（spin fluctuations）非常重要。发展更精确的理论来描述这些涨落，以解决1/χ-T曲线线性之谜，并更精确地预测居里点附近的磁性行为。
> 
> ## 七、学术思考与批判性分析
> 
> ### 1. 学术思考与深度问题
> 
> 1.  **早期量子理论的挑战与克服**：1945年时，量子力学已提出多年，但文章仍大量讨论其“冒险”和“近似”之处。这反映了早期量子理论在处理复杂**多体系统**（如固态铁磁体中海量的相互作用电子）时面临的哪些根本性挑战？这些挑战在现代物理学中是如何被逐步克服的？
>     
>     -   **思考**：早期量子力学虽成功解释了原子光谱等单体或少体问题，但在处理固态中高度相关的多体电子时，其计算复杂度呈指数级增长。主要挑战包括：**精确求解薛定谔方程**（Schrödinger equation）对于多体体系的**不可行性**；对**电子关联效应**（electron correlation effects）理解不足，导致无法构建普适性强的微观模型；以及缺乏**高效的计算工具和算法**。
>     -   **克服**：现代物理学通过以下途径逐步克服：
>         -   **近似理论的发展**：如**密度泛函理论**（Density Functional Theory, DFT）将多体问题转化为单体（或准单体）的有效势问题，大大降低了计算难度。
>         -   **多体微扰理论**：如**格林函数方法**、**费曼图方法**等，能够系统地处理电子关联效应，虽然计算复杂，但能提供更深入的物理洞察。
>         -   **强大的计算能力**：计算机技术的飞速发展（超级计算机、并行计算等）使得第一性原理计算和大规模数值模拟成为可能，能够处理更复杂的体系和更精细的模型。
>         -   **混合模型与有效理论**：结合不同理论的优势（如将DFT与动力学平均场理论DMFT结合处理强关联系统），发展针对特定现象的有效理论。
> 2.  **现象学与微观机制的平衡**：魏斯分子场理论因其简单性而广受赞誉，但其“经验性”本质和对分子场起源的“神秘”性，使其在量子力学背景下显得不足。现代凝聚态物理中，是否存在类似的“现象学成功但微观机制不明”的理论？如何平衡现象学描述与第一性原理推导？
>     
>     -   **思考**：魏斯理论的成功在于它用简单的宏观参数概括了复杂的微观相互作用，为实验结果提供了解释框架，但缺乏根本的物理洞察。在现代凝聚态物理中，类似情况仍存在，例如：
>         -   **超导现象**中的**朗道-金兹堡理论**（Landau-Ginzburg theory）：它成功描述了超导相变和临界场，但未揭示库珀对形成的微观机制（直到BCS理论）。
>         -   **高温超导**：现象学描述非常丰富，但其微观机制至今仍是物理学界的一大挑战。
>         -   **拓扑材料**：在拓扑性质的分类和宏观效应上已有大量现象学描述，但其具体微观机制和与各种相互作用的耦合仍需深入研究。
>     -   **平衡**：平衡现象学与第一性原理至关重要：
>         -   **现象学**提供直观的**宏观图像和预测**，帮助组织实验数据，并快速筛选出关键参数。它通常是理论探索的起点。
>         -   **第一性原理**（或更基础的微观理论）揭示**根本物理机制**，验证或修正现象学假设，并能预测现象学理论无法涵盖的新行为。
>         -   **最佳路径**是“**现象学指导，微观理论验证与深化**”。从现象学观察提出假设，再用微观理论探究其根源，最后通过实验检验。当微观理论过于复杂时，现象学提供了一个可行的桥梁。
> 3.  **近似的困境**：文章多次提到“收敛性差”和“近似效果不佳”的问题，尤其是在海森堡模型的更高阶近似中。这暗示了在复杂系统建模中，仅仅增加数学复杂度（例如级数展开的更高项）并不总能带来更好的物理洞察或定量准确性。那么，在理论构建中，何时应寻求更复杂的近似，何时又应考虑完全不同的模型范式？
>     
>     -   **思考**：级数展开的收敛性问题在许多物理理论中都存在，特别是在强相互作用或临界现象附近。增加更高阶项意味着试图在原有模型框架内捕获更多细节，但如果模型本身的基础近似与物理实际不符，或者系统处于高度非线性区域，更高阶项可能无法有效修正，甚至导致结果发散。
>     -   **何时寻求更复杂的近似**：
>         -   当基本物理图像（如相互作用类型、对称性）已被验证，且低阶近似已捕获了大部分物理行为，但需要更精确的定量结果时。
>         -   当系统处于弱相互作用极限，微扰展开是合理且收敛时。
>         -   当增加近似复杂性能够系统地、可控地减小误差，并能揭示新的、次要但重要的物理效应时。
>     -   **何时考虑完全不同的模型范式**：
>         -   当现有模型的基本假设与实验观察出现根本性矛盾时（如经典偶极相互作用无法解释魏斯分子场强度）。
>         -   当增加近似复杂性未能显著改善结果，甚至导致理论失效或收敛性极差时，这通常暗示着原有模型未能捕获关键的物理机制。
>         -   当有新的物理概念或实验现象出现，现有模型难以自然解释时（如斯托纳模型对非整数玻尔磁子数和额外比热的解释）。
> 4.  **模型两极与中间路线**：文章强调了海森堡和斯托纳理论的“两极”性质（束缚电子 vs. 巡游电子），并认为真实情况介于两者之间。这种“中间路线”的理念如何影响了后续凝聚态物理理论的发展，例如密度泛函理论（DFT）或多体微扰理论在处理电子关联效应时的思想？
>     
>     -   **思考**：这种“中间路线”的理念是凝聚态物理学中一个普遍且深刻的认识。它强调了真实系统复杂性，往往不是简单地由某个极端模型完全描述。
>     -   **影响**：
>         -   **DFT的兴起**：DFT在处理电子关联效应时，通过交换关联泛函（exchange-correlation functional）来近似地考虑电子间的复杂相互作用。尽管泛函形式有局限，但它在一定程度上避免了极端简化，试图通过局域或半局域近似来描述电子在原子核周围的局域性和在整个晶体中的巡游性。更高精度的泛函（如杂化泛函）甚至尝试引入部分非局域交换作用，模糊了“束缚”与“巡游”的界限。
>         -   **多体微扰理论**：例如，在Hubbard模型中，可以通过调节库仑相互作用参数**U**与带宽**W**的比例，来描述电子从巡游（U/W << 1）到局域（U/W >> 1）的转变，从而为中间路线提供理论框架。**动力学平均场理论**（Dynamical Mean-Field Theory, DMFT）就是一种处理强关联电子系统的先进方法，它将局域相关性（束缚电子特征）与巡游电子的能带理论结合起来。
>         -   **混合模型**：现代计算方法常采用“混杂”方法，例如在特定区域使用局域高精度方法（如量子化学方法），在其他区域使用能带理论。
> 5.  **钆（Gadolinium）的启示**：Gadolinium的例子表明，当某些复杂因素（如自旋轨道耦合、电子迁移）被最小化时，简单的Heisenberg模型能够“异常良好地”工作。这对于我们理解理论的适用边界和识别关键物理机制有何启示？它是否表明在某些特定体系中，简化的模型可以提供比复杂模型更深刻的物理图像？
>     
>     -   **思考**：钆的例子是一个“**反常的成功**”，它挑战了“理论越复杂越精确”的直觉。这带来了深刻的启示：
>     -   **理论的适用边界**：每个理论模型都有其**适用范围和前提假设**。当实际体系恰好满足这些前提（例如，钆的4f电子深埋，自旋-轨道耦合和迁移效应确实不重要），即使是简单的模型也能表现出惊人的准确性。这提醒研究者在应用理论时，要仔细审视其适用边界。
>     -   **识别关键物理机制**：钆的例子突出了一点——在某些情况下，通过**简化体系**（实验或理论上），可以剥离掉次要因素，从而更清晰地揭示**主导物理机制**。对于钆而言，简单的交换相互作用是核心，其他复杂性是干扰。
>     -   **深刻的物理图像**：在某些特定体系中，简化的模型确实可以提供比复杂模型**更深刻、更直观的物理图像**。因为复杂模型虽然试图包含所有细节，但可能因参数过多、计算不透明而掩盖了核心物理。一个“恰到好处”的简化模型，能够抓住主要矛盾，揭示系统的本质特征，这对于建立物理直觉和概念理解至关重要。这并不意味着复杂理论无用，而是说，在某些情况下，理解的深度可能来自于恰当的简化，而非纯粹的数学复杂性。
> 
> ### 2. 研究局限分析 (文中提及)
> 
> -   **魏斯分子场理论**：现象学本质，无法解释分子场的微观起源，预测的居里点过低，无法解释各向异性细节。
> -   **经典偶极模型**：相互作用过弱，预测各向异性与实验不符。
> -   **海森堡模型（低阶近似）**：忽略分子场波动（第一近似），条件过于宽松（允许线性链铁磁），高阶近似收敛性差，结果不满意（第二近似预测简单立方不铁磁）。
> -   **伊辛模型**：物理上不完全（忽略自旋垂直分量），仅限于低维晶格，结果具有模型依赖性，不能与真实材料行为过于紧密对应。
> -   **贝特-皮尔斯-魏斯方法**：在居里点以下区域计算困难，低温下可能不准确（未考虑长程有序和自旋波）。
> -   **布洛赫自旋波理论**：仅适用于极低温度（接近完全饱和），假设反转自旋数量很少。
> -   **斯托纳理论**：忽略电子**关联能**（correlation energy），在某些预测上与实验存在定量差异（如电子比热与T=0饱和磁化强度推导出的参数不一致）。参数（如`kθ'/ε0`，d电子总数）存在不确定性，可能导致与实验的吻合是偶然而非必然。
> -   **普遍问题**：所有理论在精确预测复杂现象（如剩磁、磁滞）方面均不足。对实验中观察到的1/χ-T曲线的近似线性关系，理论上仍是一个“谜团”。
> 
> ### 3. 拓展研究思路
>

Other methods of calculation than the series one are obviously desirable. One step in this direction has been made by Fay for a simple cubic lattice. He splits the entire crystal into elementary cubes each containing eight atoms, and within each cube the exchange interactions are taken into account rigorously by means of formulas which Serber has developed for the characteristic values of an eight-electron system. The coupling between electrons of different cubes is handled by methods equivalent to the first approximation of Heisenberg's theory. Thus the secular problem connected with (13) can be solved "half-rigorously, " i.e., taking into account the fluctuations in half of the interactions, but employing essentially mean values for the remainder, since any given atom has three neighbors within its own cube, and three in other cubes. Fay finds it makes comparatively little difference whether the problem is treated "half-rigorously, " or whether the entire problem is handled by the first approximation, where only the average fields are used throughout. Namely, the formula for the Curie point becomes Tc = 2.55J/k instead of Tq=3J/k, and the (1/y) —T curves above the Curie point are nearly straight lines. Thus incorporation of half the interactions rigorously does not lead to anything like as much deviation from the simple formulas of the original Weiss theory as Heisenberg's Gaussian calculation would suggest. Another way in which the Heisenberg model can be simplified is by changing the interaction potential. This is done in the so-called Ising model, which uses a coupling energy of the form -2J szi szj rather than -2J Si·Sj as in (13). We use the notation s rather than S as it is assumed that there is only one free spin per atom. In a certain sense the Ising model is a purely mathematical fiction, as it neglects the interactions -2J(sxi sxj + syi syj) between the components of spin perpendicular to the direction of the magnetic field, which are often important physically. There is a considerable literature on the calculations of crystalline characteristic values and Curie points with the Ising model. Such treatments have the great merit of being clearcut and rigorous for the assumed problem. > # THE BETHE-PEIERLS-WEISS METHOD
> 
> 贝特-佩尔斯-外斯方法 **Bethe-Peierls-Weiss Method**: 贝特-佩尔斯-外斯方法，一种处理合金中有序-无序相变问题的方法，后被用于计算铁磁体的居里点和磁化率。

> Another and quite different procedure has recently been developed by Peter Weiss, a young American not to be confused with the French physicist Pierre Weiss, the father of the molecular field, who died in 1940. The essential feature of this new calculation, not yet published because of war delays, is that it adapts to the case of ferromagnetism a method which had previously been devised by Bethe and Peierls in the order-disorder problem encountered in the structure of alloys. The method has both power and generality. Unlike Fay's treatment, for instance, it is not confined to the simple cubic lattice. Peierls himself showed how it could be adapted to magnetic susceptibilities by a simple change of notation in the order-disorder problem, provided one employed the Ising rather than Heisenberg model. Weiss, on the other hand, uses the true Heisenberg model, and with the latter, a calculation of the Bethe-Peierls type is both more difficult and more interesting than for the Ising potential, since it involves the quantum mechanics of angular momentum vectors, instead of classical scalar quantities. 最近，另一位年轻的美国人彼得·外斯发展了一种完全不同的方法，不要把他与1940年去世的法国物理学家、分子场之父皮埃尔·外斯混淆。这项由于战争延误而尚未发表的新计算，其基本特征是将贝特和佩尔斯早先在合金结构的有序-无序问题中设计的方法应用于铁磁性情况。该方法既有威力又有普遍性。例如，与费伊的处理不同，它不局限于简单立方晶格。佩尔斯本人展示了如何通过有序-无序问题中符号的简单变更，将其应用于磁化率，前提是采用伊辛模型而非海森堡模型。另一方面，外斯使用了真正的海森堡模型，对于后者，贝特-佩尔斯类型的计算比在伊辛势能下更困难，也更有趣，因为它涉及角动量矢量的量子力学，而不是经典的标量。

> The central idea of the procedure of Weiss is to consider a given atom i and its z nearest neighbors i+1, ..., i+z as a structural unit U'. The interactions involving atom i are thus taken into account rigorously. The atoms i+1, i+2, ..., i+z form a shell around i. Their coupling with each other and with the central atom i is treated rigorously, inasmuch as a quantum-mechanical system of z+1 spins is a soluble problem if z is not too large. It is, however, necessary to assume that the spin quantum number of each atom is 1/2, because the problem becomes too complicated with more than one magnetic electron per atom. The coupling of atoms i+1, ..., i+z with atoms exterior to the shell U is included by means of an approximation. It is assumed that the influence of the outside atoms can be represented by the addition of a potential energy term in the partition function for the system of z+1 atoms. The magnitude of this term is determined by the requirement that the mean magnetic moment of the central atom i be the same as that of any atom i+1, ..., i+z in the shell surrounding i, since in the crystal all atoms are strictly equivalent on the average. Insertion of the extra term to allow for coupling of atoms exterior to U is equivalent to supposing that the outer atoms i+1, ..., i+z of U are subject not merely to the applied field H, but rather to the sum H+He of H and an effective field He. On the other hand, the central atom i is subject only to H, since the interaction with its neighbors has been taken into account rigorously, and it is for the latter reason that the Bethe-Peierls attack is such a good approximation. The effective field He is considerably more general than the molecular field qM used in the classical Weiss theory, and is not to be confused with the latter. In the first place He need not be linear in M, although this is the case above the Curie point, and even when He is a linear function He = αM of M, the coefficient of proportionality α is not assumed to be independent of the temperature T. > # THE BLOCH SPIN WAVES
> 
> 布洛赫自旋波 **Bloch spin waves**: 布洛赫自旋波，铁磁体在低温下的一种集体激发，表现为自旋偏转在晶格中的传播。
> 
> Fortunately, another method, particularly adapted to the low temperature region, was developed some time ago by Bloch. The starting point of his attack is Slater's observation that the characteristic values of the Heisenberg exchange coupling can be rigorously determined if the spins of all but one atom are parallel (i.e., N-1 atoms have Mz = +1/2, and one has Mz = -1/2). The solutions can be interpreted as representing waves of disturbance in which the reversed spin is propagated through the crystal with various possible wave-lengths. The fundamental hypothesis of the Bloch calculation is that if there are k reversed spins, the solution can be obtained by additive superposition of k solutions in which a single spin is reversed. This will be an allowable approximation only if k is small compared with N, so that the probability of two or more reversed spins being at the same point of the lattice is small. (When reversed spins coincide, the approximation is meaningless, as one cannot turn a northward spin southward more than once. ) Hence the Bloch method of calculation with the Heisenberg model is only satisfactory in the immediate neighborhood of complete saturation M=Np, and consequently only at temperatures very low compared to the Curie point. > It is to be emphasized that all the calculations with the Heisenberg theory which we have discussed in the preceding pages are based on the Heitler-London model. The latter represents a completely non-polar approximation or, in other words, it supposes that the electrons responsible for ferromagnetism always remain on the same atom and do not participate in electrical conduction. This is an idealization never completely realized in fact. Another limiting case is furnished by the model of Sommerfeld, Wigner, Seitz, and others analogous to that of Hund and Mulliken in the theory of valence. In this model of "itinerant electrons, " one supposes that the 3d electrons circulate independently and freely from one atom to another. The resulting momentary shortage or surplus of charge on any particular atom makes the crystal instantaneously polar. Theoretical calculations from this point of view have been made by Bloch, by Slater and especially by Stoner. Evidently, the truth is somewhere between the Heitler-London model and that of itinerant electrons. The actual intermediate case is too difficult to calculate. Hence Stoner's calculations fulfill a very useful role in rounding out the theory, for if we have available the predictions of theory for the approximation of completely free as well as completely bound electrons then we can see qualitatively by interpolation what is to be expected between the two limiting cases. When, as fortunately often proves true, the results are quite similar in the two extremes, we can be reasonably confident that the behavior is not very different in the intermediate region. The calculations of Stoner are based on a welldefined, clear-cut model, and so have a certain characteristic elegance. It is assumed that the electron energy levels are attributes of the whole crystal rather than the individual atom, and can be handled by the Fermi-Dirac statistics. A molecular field is used to represent the exchange interaction, which is hence treated in a somewhat phenomenological fashion, and no more completely or rigorously than in the first approximation of the Heisenberg theory. The Stoner procedure can hence be characterised as the superposition of the Weiss molecular field on the Sommerfeld theory of electronic conduction, and depicts what is called "collective electron ferromagnetism. > # COMPARATIVE MERITS OF THE HEISENBERG AND STONER THEORIES
> 
> 海森堡理论与斯托纳理论的比较
> 
> One is apt to wonder whether from the agreement with experiment or other considerations one can deduce whether the non-polar Heisenberg model or the polar Stoner one, comes closest to reality. In our opinion, it is impossible to say anything very definite on this subject. One immediately recognizes that there are a good many features common to both theories. For example, both give a reasonable behavior of the saturation below the Curie point (item (2) above). The mode of approach to the magnetization at the absolute zero is, however, represented somewhat better by the Heisenberg model (cf. item (5)). At first thought, it may seem that a non-polar theory is incapable of offering any explanation of the excess specific heat, or of the fact that the magnetization at the absolute zero is not an integral number of Bohr magnetons per atom (items (1) and (6)). This is no longer true if one uses a slight variant of the Heisenberg theory in which not all atoms have a free spin. As a typical case, let us consider nickel. Here the usual Heisenberg model with one spin per atom is to be identified with a shortage rather than surplus of one electron compared with a closed shell, in other words with the configuration d⁸ rather than d⁹. Now, all told, there are 10 electrons per atom in nickel in addition to the electrons in the interior part 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶ of the atom which are so tightly bound that they can be omitted from consideration. These 10 electrons have their choice of being housed in 4s or 3d states. The neutral Ni atom has the configuration 4s²3d⁸, indicating that lower energy is obtained by occupancy of the two 4s places than by filling out the 3d shell to the completed group 3d¹⁰. In the solid however, the 4s wave functions of adjacent atoms will overlap considerably, and as a result the totality of 4s states for the crystal will be spread into a quite wide band, which, is presumably broad compared with the 3d band. As 4s² configurations are generally not ferromagnetic, the spins will be paired in the portion of the 4s band which is occupied, and hence will not contribute to the magnetism. Probably the 4s band is so wide that the upper portion of it is higher than the 3d band, which may in first approximation be treated as infinitely narrow in comparison. If we assume that 70 percent of the 4s band is above the 3d band, then the most stable configuration will be that in which on the average 0.6 of an electron per atom is in a 4s state, and 9.4 electrons in 3d, so that symbolically the configuration is 4s⁰·⁶3d⁹·⁴. The fact that the saturation magnetization at T = 0 is .6 Bohr magneton per atom is then explained without the need of invoking the Stoner mechanism in the intermediate case shown in Fig. 5. How about the excess specific heat with this picture? The energy is undoubtedly different when two 3d⁹ atoms are adjacent than when a 3d⁹ is next to a 3d¹⁰. There will hence be a specific heat associated with the redistribution of the 3d⁹ and 3d¹⁰ states among the different lattice sites when the temperature is varied. Sixty percent of the total number of atoms are always to be kept in the configuration 3d⁹, and forty percent in 3d¹⁰, but there are an enormous variety of ways in which the two varieties of atoms can be peppered about in the crystal. This effect is rather hard to treat in a quantitative way, although some preliminary calculations have been made by H. Hurwitz. It is not known yet, for instance, whether the resulting specific heat is linear in the temperature, or whether the agreement on the order of magnitude of the parameters as determined by the various criteria is as good as discussed in item (7) for the Stoner theory. Still, Hurwitz shows that it is probable that the specific heat is not vastly different in order of magnitude from that furnished by the Stoner collective electron mechanism. Incidently if we had a pure Heisenberg model with one free spin per atom, there would still be some specific heat, due to the spectrum of energy states for the exchange energy of the crystal, or in other words due to the fluctuations in the molecular field. At low temperatures this specific heat can be treated by the Bloch spin-wave method. It is, however, proportional to T^(3/2) and so not large enough to account for the observed excess specific heat, which is linear in T. Our suggestion of a lattice built on 3d⁹ and 3d¹⁰ atoms is in a certain sense a compromise between the simple Heisenberg picture using only one type of atom and the Stoner model of freely migrating electrons. The latter neglects the interplay between the various electrons, so that it is possible for large surpluses or deficits of charge to pile up on a given atom, which can hence be regarded as momentarily existing in configurations such as 3d⁷, 3d⁸, 3d⁹, etc. In other words the Stoner theory neglects what Wigner and Seitz call the correlation energy. In our opinion the correlation effect is a rather large one, as the indications are that the spread in 3d levels due to inter-atomic overlap is smaller than the higher ionization potentials for removal of several electrons which would have to be overcome in order for the electrons to migrate freely through the crystal without much regard to one another. In other words, our generalization of the Heisenberg-Heitler-London model does not involve the high degree of instantaneous polarity found in the unmodified collective electron viewpoint, and so perhaps comes closer to the truth. In the last analysis, it is quibbling to try and say which idea is right, since the addition of appropriate correction terms, viz. the states of higher ionization in the former and correlation effects in the latter, ultimately merge the two together. Incidentally, there is, as far as we can see, no reason why in the Stoner model the number of 3d electrons per atom, or rather number of holes in the 3d shell per atom, need be taken as unity. The unit value assumed by Stoner applies only if the 4s shell is half filled, so that the average configuration is 4s¹3d⁹. This will be the case if the 4s band splitting is rather symmetrical, so that about half of the 4s band is above, and half below the 3d band. However, there is no reason why this should necessarily be true. With two undetermined parameters, viz. the total number of d electrons and the ratio kθ'/ε0, the extended Stoner theory would have so much leeway in the way of undetermined constants that it would be hard to tell whether any agreement obtained with experiment would be significant or fortuitous. The fact that the original Heisenberg theory has no undetermined constants when reduced units are used must be regarded as both an element of strength and weakness in this theory — strength in that any agreement achieved with experiment is not the result of an ad hoc choice of constants, but weakness in that not enough flexibility is included to allow for the complicated structure of a real solid. Namely, with any type of theory one always neglects certain corrections or perturbations the inclusion of which will presumably introduce more curvature. Among the various complications which affect the relation between 1/x and T are the following: (a) the fact that exchange coupling cannot be represented rigorously by the usual molecular field. In other words there exist fluctuations in the latter which are not covered by the conventional first approximation of the Heisenberg theory; (b) distortion due to the remains of spin-orbit coupling which persist despite quenching of most of the orbital angular momentum in a solid, and which interfere with the freedom of spin orientation; (c) in an ionic model, the migrations of electrons through the crystal; (d) in a generalized Heitler-London model, the redistribution of the configurations d⁹ and d¹⁰ among the different lattice sites. The calculations of Weiss with the Bethe-Peierls method allow in principle for (a), and those of Stoner include (c), but not (a). None of the existing calculations have considered (b) in any detail. All told, the observed amount of linearity in the 1/x —T relations must be regarded as something of a mystery. > # QUESTION OF THE SIGN OF THE EXCHANGE INTEGRAL
> 
> 交换积分符号的问题
> 
> In order to have ferromagnetism, it is essential that the exchange integral defined by (10) be positive. Otherwise the exchange effects tend to destroy rather than amplify the magnetic moment, or in other words the constant of the molecular field is negative and has a demagnetizing effect. Hitherto in this article we have tacitly omitted examination of whether the exchange integral had the proper sign to produce ferromagnetism. Obviously it would be desirable if one could calculate this sign theoretically, for then one would be able to predict whether a given material might be ferromagnetic. We use the word "might" rather than "is" because the work of Bloch and Stoner shows that even with a favorable sign of the exchange integral, ferromagnetism is suppressed if the spreading of energy levels due to electron migration is too large. Unfortunately it is so difficult to calculate exchange integrals in ferromagnetic materials that this has not been done. Even for electrons belonging to free hydrogenic atoms the computation of these integrals would be laborious, and undoubtedly interatomic forces distort the d electrons so badly that conclusions based on calculations with free atoms would probably not be reliable, at least without some further analysis. There is, however, one working rule, first developed by Slater, which seems to furnish a rather convenient qualitative index as to whether ferromagnetism is possible. It states that ferromagnetism can exist only if the atomic radius is small compared to the interatomic distance. Furthermore, Sommerfeld and Bethe have shown that the Slater rule has a simple, though rather crude quantummechanical explanation, as follows. The bulk of the contribution to the integral (10) presumably comes from regions, in the six-dimensional coordinate space, which are exterior to the outermost node of the wave functions of both electrons, at least provided the azimuthal quantum number is zero or small. The contribution of the region inside the nodes is considered of minor significance by Sommerfeld and Bethe because the wave functions will not overlap sufficiently to penetrate strongly the interior portion of a neighboring atom and also to a certain extent because the volume covered by the interior regions of the atom is small. Thus, though this is perhaps the weakest part of the argument, the dominant part of the integral is considered to arise from regions for which the factor φi*(1)φj*(2)φi(2)φj(1) is positive, so that the sign behavior of the integral can be gauged by examining the sign of K. After allowing for terms that cancel out because φi, φj satisfy respectively the wave equations for free atoms i and j respectively, it is found that the significant parts of K are those corresponding to the potential energy due to the nucleus of the other atom, with allowance for screening by non-valence electrons, and to the mutual potential energy of the two electrons involved in the exchange integral. If the nuclear attractive term predominates over the inter-electronic repulsive term, the factor K will be essentially negative; otherwise it will be positive. Now if the radius of the atom is at all comparable with the interatomic distance, there will be appreciable overlapping of the wave functions, i.e., a significant factor φi*(1)φj*(2)φi(2)φj(1), at points where the interelectronic repulsion is overshadowed by the nuclear term inasmuch as a nucleus contains many electronic charges. On the other hand, when the atomic radius is very small compared with the interatomic distance, the only appreciable overlapping will be in a more or less void space midway between the two atoms, where the influence of both atomic centers is unimportant and the important energetic term arises only because of the possibility of the two electrons themselves sometimes being close together in this midway region. Hence one can see qualitatively why in order for the repulsive terms to outweigh the nuclear effects and give a positive exchange integral, one must satisfy the Slater criterion that the atomic radius be small compared to the interatomic distance. The Sommerfeld-Bethe argument, moreover, goes further and indicates why ferromagnetism is found only for d and f electrons and never for s electrons. Namely, the atomic wave functions contain a factor r^l, where l is the azimuthal quantum number. In s-states the absence of a node at the nucleus presumably makes the negative contribution to the exchange integral predominate if the atomic diameter is not completely negligible compared to the inter-atomic distance, so that there is some overlapping. Hence we can understand why s-electrons never give ferromagnetism. On the other hand, in d- or f-states the factor r^l makes the integrand small in the interior of an atom where the nuclear forces are greatest. In order for there to be ferromagnetism it is not sufficient just that the exchange integral be positive, but in addition it must be sufficiently large to predominate over any energy band structure due to electron migration (cf. Fig. 5). The factor r^l is helpful not merely because it enhances the likelihood of a positive sign, but also because it will make the integral larger once it has become positive, by accentuating the outer regions where the interelectronic repulsion is the prime effect. We have not said anything about p electrons, but clearly it would be optimism to expect this very qualitative argument to provide us with any definite predictions regarding this intermediate case. 为了具有铁磁性，由(10)式定义的交换积分必须为正，这一点至关重要。否则，交换效应倾向于破坏而不是增强磁矩，换句话说，分子场常数为负，具有退磁效应。到目前为止，我们在本文中一直默认省略了对交换积分是否具有产生铁磁性的正确符号的检查。显然，如果人们能从理论上计算出这个符号，那将是可取的，因为那样就能预测给定的材料是否可能具有铁磁性。我们使用“可能”而不是“是”这个词，是因为布洛赫和斯托纳的工作表明，即使交换积分符号有利，如果由于电子迁移导致的能级展宽太大，铁磁性也会被抑制。不幸的是，计算铁磁材料中的交换积分非常困难，以至于尚未完成。即使对于属于自由氢原子性质的电子，计算这些积分也会很费力，而且毫无疑问，原子间力会严重扭曲d电子，以至于基于自由原子计算得出的结论很可能不可靠，至少在没有进一步分析的情况下是如此。然而，有一个由斯莱特首先提出的工作定则，它似乎为判断铁磁性是否可能提供了一个相当方便的定性指标。它表明，只有当原子半径与原子间距相比很小时，铁磁性才可能存在。此外，索末菲和贝特已经证明，斯莱特定则有一个简单但相当粗糙的量子力学解释，如下所述。对积分(10)的主要贡献大概来自六维坐标空间中，两个电子波函数的最外层节点之外的区域，至少当角量子数为零或很小时是这样。索末菲和贝特认为节点内部区域的贡献具有次要意义，因为波函数不会充分交叠以强烈穿透相邻原子的内部部分，并且在某种程度上也因为原子内部区域所覆盖的体积很小。因此，尽管这也许是论证中最薄弱的部分，但积分的主要部分被认为是来自因子 φi*(1)φj*(2)φi(2)φj(1) 为正的区域，因此可以通过检查K的符号来判断积分的符号行为。考虑到由于φi, φj分别满足自由原子i和j的波动方程而抵消的项之后，发现K的重要部分对应于由另一个原子核产生的势能（考虑到非价电子的屏蔽）以及交换积分中涉及的两个电子的相互势能。如果核吸引项超过电子间排斥项，因子K将基本上是负的；否则它将是正的。现在，如果原子半径与原子间距完全可以比较，那么在电子间排斥力被核项遮蔽的点（因为一个原子核包含许多电子电荷），波函数将有可观的重叠，即一个显著的因子 φi*(1)φj*(2)φi(2)φj(1)。另一方面，当原子半径与原子间距相比非常小时，唯一明显的重叠将出现在两个原子之间或多或少是空的空间中，在那里，两个原子中心的影响都不重要，重要的能量项仅仅是因为两个电子本身有时会在这个中间区域靠近的可能性而产生的。因此，人们可以定性地理解，为什么为了使排斥项超过核效应并给出一个正的交换积分，必须满足斯莱特判据，即原子半径小于原子间距。此外，索末菲-贝特的论证更进一步，并指出了为什么铁磁性只在d和f电子中发现，而从未在s电子中发现。也就是说，原子波函数包含一个因子 r^l，其中l是角量子数。在s态中，原子核处没有节点，如果原子直径与原子间距相比并非完全可忽略，从而存在一些重叠的话，这大概会使对交换积分的负贡献占主导地位。因此，我们可以理解为什么s电子永远不会产生铁磁性。另一方面，在d或f态中，因子 r^l 使被积函数在核力最强的原子内部变小。为了存在铁磁性，仅仅交换积分为正是不够的，此外，它还必须足够大，以克服由于电子迁移引起的任何能带结构（参见图5）。因子 r^l 不仅因为它增加了正号的可能性，而且因为它会使积分在变为正之后变得更大，通过突出电子间排斥力是主要效应的外部区域，因此是有帮助的。我们没有对p电子说任何话，但显然，期望这个非常定性的论证能为我们提供关于这个中间情况的任何明确预测，那是过于乐观了。

> # FEEBLE PARAMAGNETISM
> 
> 弱顺磁性 **Feeble paramagnetism**: 弱顺磁性，指磁化率远小于正常顺磁体，且几乎不随温度变化的顺磁性。
> 
> From the preceding discussion it is apparent that the large positive exchange integrals necessary for ferromagnetism will be realized only under rather special favorable conditions. Normally the exchange integral will be negative, and the spins will tend to set themselves anti-parallel. This is the situation ordinarily encountered for the electrons responsible for chemical bonding. In fact, the usual theories of valence, electronpairing, etc., are founded on the idea that a saturated chemical bond corresponds to the compounding of the individual atomic spins to a zero resultant molecular spin. This is just the reverse of the state of affairs necessary for ferromagnetism, since the latter demands that the configuration of lowest energy be one of high rather than compensated spin. Thus ferromagnetism may be regarded as a sort of exception to the usual valence rules. For the normal case of negative exchange integrals, the effect of exchange is demagnetizing, and may roughly be described by taking the Curie temperature Tc to be negative in the Weiss Curie formula x = C/(T - Tc), since Tc has the same sign as the exchange integral. If the exchange coupling is powerful enough so that -Tc is large compared with T, then the susceptibility will be independent of temperature, and much smaller than for a free atom, where Tc=0. Thus a mechanism is provided for understanding why many substances, far outnumbering the strongly paramagnetic, or ferromagnetic ones, have a feeble paramagnetism which is more or less independent of temperature. In a feebly paramagnetic material, the spin of an individual atom can be as large as in a highly magnetic substance, but the exchange effects are demagnetizing and tend to make the resultant spin of the entire crystal small. A typical metal of this type is aluminum, which is only weakly paramagnetic despite the fact that the normal state of the Al atom is ²P. Strictly speaking, any discussion of feeble paramagnetism is outside the scope of a report on ferromagnetism. However, weakly paramagnetic materials are so common, and the theoretical explanation of their behavior ties in so closely with that of ferromagnetics, that before closing it has seemed worth while to comment on them briefly. We should mention that there are other effects besides negative exchange coupling which enfeeble paramagnetism. In particular there is the suppression of paramagnetism due to the spreading of energy levels caused by the migration of the electrons through a solid conductor. Pauli first showed that this dispersion of translational energy levels removes most of the spin paramagnetism when there is no exchange coupling, and we have seen that Bloch and Stoner have demonstrated that this can still be true even when the exchange integrals have a sign favorable to ferromagnetism, provided they are not too large. Since the wave functions for s and p electrons usually overlap adjacent atoms considerably, the migration effects are probably sufficient, regardless of the sign of the exchange integrals, to suppress most of the paramagnetism in solids not having d or f electrons. Special mention should be made of the fact that in the nonferromagnetic elements of the transition groups, the paramagnetism, though still of what may be called the "feeble" variety, is considerably larger than usual for this category. Precisely this behavior is to be expected theoretically, since the wave functions of d electrons overlap other atoms less than do those of the s or p type, and in consequence there is a narrower band structure, with less suppression of paramagnetism. For a very interesting description of the experimental data on this subject, data on the widths of the energy bands, etc., the reader is referred particularly to the discussion in Mott and Jones' book. So far we have cited only feeble paramagnetism which arises because of nearly completing quenching of the magnetic moments of atoms which are paramagnetic when free. Sometimes distortion due to interatomic forces can originate a weak paramagnetism in materials whose isolated atoms or ions are diamagnetic. This situation is more likely to arise in an ionic salt than in a conductor, inasmuch as electrons of ions in closed shells usually migrate but little. Examples are probably furnished by Sc₂O₃, TiO₂, CeO₂, and numerous cobaltamines. 从前面的讨论可以明显看出，铁磁性所需的大正交换积分，只会在相当特殊且有利的条件下才能实现。通常情况下，交换积分将是负的，自旋将倾向于使自己排列成反平行。这是负责化学键合的电子通常遇到的情况。事实上，通常的价键理论、电子配对等，都建立在饱和化学键对应于单个原子自旋组合成零总分子自旋的思想之上。这与铁磁性所需的状况正好相反，因为后者要求最低能量构型是高自旋的，而不是补偿自旋的。因此，铁磁性可以被视为对通常价键规则的一种例外。对于负交换积分的正常情况，交换效应是退磁的，并且可以通过在外斯居里公式 x = C/(T - Tc) 中将居里温度Tc取为负值来粗略描述，因为Tc与交换积分具有相同的符号。如果交换耦合足够强，以至于 -Tc 与 T 相比较大，那么磁化率将与温度无关，并且比自由原子（Tc=0）的磁化率小得多。这样就提供了一个机制，用于理解为什么许多物质（其数量远远超过强顺磁性或铁磁性物质）具有或多或少与温度无关的弱顺磁性。在弱顺磁性材料中，单个原子的自旋可以像在强磁性物质中一样大，但交换效应是退磁的，并倾向于使整个晶体的总自旋变小。这类金属的一个典型例子是铝，它只是弱顺磁性的，尽管铝原子的正常态是²P。严格地说，任何关于弱顺磁性的讨论都超出了铁磁性报告的范围。然而，弱顺磁性材料是如此普遍，并且对其行为的理论解释与铁磁体的解释如此紧密相连，以至于在结束之前，似乎值得对其简要评论一下。我们应该提到，除了负交换耦合之外，还有其他效应会减弱顺磁性。特别是，由于电子在固体导体中迁移导致的能级展宽，会抑制顺磁性。泡利首先证明，当没有交换耦合时，这种平动能级的色散消除了大部分自旋顺磁性，而我们已经看到，布洛赫和斯托纳证明了，即使交换积分具有有利于铁磁性的符号，只要它们不是太大，这也可能仍然成立。由于s和p电子的波函数通常与相邻原子有相当大的重叠，迁移效应很可能足以抑制不含d或f电子的固体中的大部分顺磁性，无论交换积分的符号如何。应该特别提及这样一个事实，即在过渡族的非铁磁性元素中，顺磁性虽然仍属于可被称为“弱”的类型，但比此类别的通常情况要大得多。这种行为正是理论上所预期的，因为d电子的波函数与其他原子的重叠程度小于s或p型电子，因此能带结构更窄，对顺磁性的抑制更少。对于关于这个主题的实验数据、能带宽度数据等非常有趣的描述，请读者特别参考莫特和琼斯书中的讨论。到目前为止，我们只引用了弱顺磁性，这种弱顺磁性是由于本应在自由状态下具有顺磁性的原子的磁矩几乎完全猝灭而产生的。有时，原子间力引起的畸变可以在其孤立原子或离子是抗磁性的材料中产生弱顺磁性。这种情况更可能发生在离子盐中，而不是在导体中，因为闭壳层中离子的电子通常很少迁移。例子可能由Sc₂O₃, TiO₂, CeO₂和许多钴胺化合物提供。

> # ANTI-FERROMAGNETISM
> 
> 反铁磁性 **Anti-ferromagnetism**: 反铁磁性，指相邻原子或离子的磁矩反向平行排列，使得总磁矩为零的磁性状态。
> 
> In the preceding discussion we have stressed how difficult it is to know whether feeble paramagnetism is to be attributed to negative exchange integrals rather than to some of the other mechanisms which we have mentioned. There is, however, one class of materials, known as "anti-ferromagnetics" in which it is quite clear that the suppression of paramagnetism is to be identified with exchange coupling. These substances, which are not very common, have a susceptibility which passes through a maximum when the temperature is raised, as shown in Fig. 6. On the other hand, ordinary feeble paramagnetism is characterized by a susceptibility which is more or less independent of temperature, but which usually decreases monotonically and slowly as the temperature is raised. Examples of anti-ferromagnetic media include MnO, MnSe, Cr₂O₃, and CrSb. The peculiar behavior illustrated in Fig. 6 is nicely explained theoretically in the following way. Suppose that we have a crystal whose constituent atoms can be resolved into two sublattices A and B such that the nearest neighbors of the atoms of A are atoms belonging to B, and vice versa. The simple cubic and body-centered cubic lattices are both of this type. In our rudimentary derivation of the Weiss molecular field from the exchange potential (8), we assumed that on the average the spins of all atoms pointed in the same direction. This supposition is admissible if the exchange integral is positive, as then the state of lowest exchange energy is that of complete parallelism. With a negative integral, however, the exchange energy of two atoms is a minimum if their spins are antiparallel. Hence the configuration of deepest energy for the crystal as a whole is that in which the spins of sublattice A all point northward, and those of B all southward or vice versa. Consequently if a method based on mean values is used, the theory should be generalized by assuming that the mean direction of the spin in sublattice A is the opposite from that in B, apart from polarization effects due to an external applied field. With this generalized model, a "staggered" non-vanishing molecular field can exist which tends to direct the spins of A in one direction and those of B in the other. We shall omit the mathematical analysis, which is due to Neel, Bitter, and the writer. It yields the result that there is a certain critical or Curie temperature above which the spontaneous field of this character disappears. With similarly rather than oppositely directed spins for the two sublattices, no spontaneous field would have been possible at any temperature. If one treats the staggered model by a method of approximation analogous to the usual first-order one of the Heisenberg theory, then it turns out that the formula for the Curie temperature above which the staggered molecular field disappears is precisely the same as that (15) obtained for the ferromagnetic rather than anti-ferromagnetic case, except that now one must use the absolute value of the exchange integral. (Without use of the absolute value, the critical temperature would be negative, and hence meaningless. ) As the temperature is lowered, the inner field becomes stronger and stronger, and so it is harder for the field to make the spins all point in one direction when they want to be arranged alternately in opposite directions. Hence, without going through the analysis, one can see qualitatively why the susceptibility diminishes as the temperature is lowered from the Curie temperature to the absolute zero. Above the Curie temperature, on the other hand, the staggering effect is washed out, and the susceptibility decreases with increasing temperature, obeying the familiar formula C/(T+θ) (θ>0) obtainable even without introducing interlocking lattices. At the absolute zero, the inhibiting effect of the powerful internal fields on any change of alignment due to a weak external field is complete only if the latter has, apart from sense, the same direction as the former. If applied perpendicular to the alternating inner fields, the external field can still give rise to an outstanding moment by twisting slightly the orientations of the elementary magnets. Since the molecular field is presumably directed at random, the parallel and perpendicular cases, considered respectively by Bitter and Neel, should be weighted in the ratio 1:2, and it is for this reason that the susceptibility χ0 at the absolute zero for weak fields is theoretically 2/3 that χc at the Curie point. The observed values of χ0/χc, instead of being 0.67, range from 0.3 to 0.85, so that the agreement is qualitative rather than quantitative. It is especially to be emphasized that in a truly ferromagnetic medium a comparatively weak field will suffice to orient the moments of the elementary domains and make them parallel to the applied field, producing so-called saturation. On the other hand, in an anti-ferromagnetic material, even a very powerful field will produce but little rotation of the domain alignments, as the low susceptibility makes the energy of the crystal rather insensitive to external fields. One can understand qualitatively why the observations of Bizette, Squire, and Tsai on MnO shown in Fig. 6 reveal a somewhat higher susceptibility, below the Curie point, for a field of 24,000 gauss than for one of 7,000 gauss. Namely, in an anti-ferromagnetic medium a very powerful field will rotate the elementary domains into more favorable alignment, i.e., make the molecular field of more domains perpendicular to the applied field, instead of being random. In the limiting case of complete perpendicularity, the susceptibility below the Curie temperature Tc is independent of temperature and has the same value as at Tc. The rather mild dependence of susceptibility on field strength shown in Fig. 6 is to be contrasted with the behavior of a truly ferromagnetic material where a field of less than 7000 gauss would have produced complete parallelism. When the susceptibility is finite but still is somewhat influenced by field strength, one should expect some weak hysteresis phenomena, and in MnS a feeble hysteresis is indeed observed, wherein the loop is so narrow that it encloses but little area. In the preceding paragraphs we have perhaps inadvertently given the impression that feebly paramagnetic and anti-ferromagnetic media represent two distinct cases. Actually there is no sharp dividing line between the two, though a convenient criterion for anti-ferromagnetism is a maximum susceptibility at a particular temperature. Also our statement at the beginning of the discussion on feeble paramagnetism that the temperature dependence of susceptibility is of the form C/(T+ |Tc|) in the first approximation comparable with the Heisenberg theory is seen to be not really correct below the Curie point if a division into alternating sublattices is possible, for then one has a behavior of the type shown in Fig. 6. In either case, however, the variation with temperature is small, in fact less with the situation covered by Fig. 6, which gives χ0 = 2/3 χc, rather than χ0 = 1/2 χc. The fact that most weakly paramagnetic materials do not show the peculiar maximum in χ may mean that the crystal structure is not such as to permit the requisite division into sublattices, or much more likely, that the effects of electron migration are appreciable. With the Stoner collective electron mechanism, localization on individual atoms is lost, and no molecular field of consequence is possible as long as the mean exchange integral is negative, since staggering requires local order. Here we thus have a case where the results are different with the approximations of bound and itinerant electrons, and the former must be closer to the truth when a behavior such as that shown in Fig. 6 is realized. > # CONCLUSION

> 结论
> 
> In closing we should like to stress once more the great parallelism in many respects between the theory of ferromagnetism and the theory of valence. Both topics are readily tractable only for models corresponding to the limiting cases in which the electrons are treated respectively as tightly bound on individual atoms and as wandering freely from atom to atom without cognizance of each others' positions. The original Heisenberg theory of magnetism is the analogue of the Heitler-London-Slater-Pauling theory of valence, while the Stoner treatment resembles the Hund-Mulliken approach. In both subjects the truth is doubtless intermediate between the two extremes, and the results in the limiting cases usually do not differ too much from each other, giving one some confidence in a similar behavior in the intermediate region. In both valence and ferromagnetism, the agreement with experiment is often surprisingly good in the first approximation and in many cases spoiled by inclusion of the second or third approximation, leaving it something of a mystery why so nearly the first-order result would presumably be restored were it possible to carry through the calculations rigorously. Along with the similarity that we have noted, there are two main differences between the theory of ferromagnetism and that of the chemical bond. In the first place, the former requires a positive rather than negative exchange integral, in order that the energy be lowest for parallel rather than anti-parallel spin alignment. Secondly, in magnetism the entire crystal rather than the molecule is the structural unit, and consequently the potential mathematical and physical complexity is greater than for even the worst organic molecule. Has the quantum theory of ferromagnetism been a success? The answer depends to a large extent on whether one takes the attitude of the optimist or the pessimist, that is to say, whether one concentrates on the successes of the theory or looks for the flaws and emphasizes all the difficulties of rigor and convergence. Analogous remarks apply to the quantum theory of valence. If one demands a theory that will predict in advance just which alloys will be ferromagnetic, what quantitatively will be the remanence, hysteresis, etc., then one must admit failure, although as time progresses, greater refinement of the theory will perhaps increase its usefulness for qualitative prognostication. Similarly one is doomed to disappointment in the present applications of quantum mechanics to chemistry if one expects advance information, say, on just which substitutions in a benzene ring will give particular physical and chemical properties such as, say, that of photographic developer, etc. If, on the other hand, what one desires of a theory is a qualitative understanding of why nature works as it does, and the satisfying confidence of having a mechanism which would, no doubt, explain even the most complicated phenomena if the difficult mathematical calculations could be made, then the quantum theory of ferromagnetism or of valence is certainly a success. 最后，我们想再次强调铁磁性理论与价键理论在许多方面的极大相似性。这两个主题都只有在对应于极限情况的模型下才容易处理，在这些极限情况下，电子分别被视为紧紧地束缚在单个原子上，以及被视为在原子间自由游荡而不顾及彼此的位置。最初的海森堡磁性理论是海特勒-伦敦-斯莱特-鲍林价键理论的类比，而斯托纳的处理方法则类似于洪德-马利肯的方法。在这两个主题中，真相无疑介于两个极端之间，并且极限情况下的结果通常彼此相差不太大，这使得人们对中间区域类似的行为有了一些信心。在价键和铁磁性两者中，第一级近似与实验的一致性常常好得惊人，而在许多情况下，这种一致性被包含第二或第三级近似所破坏，留下了一个谜，即如果能够严格地完成计算，为什么几乎可以肯定会恢复第一级近似的果。除了我们已经注意到的相似性之外，铁磁性理论和化学键理论之间还有两个主要区别。首先，前者要求交换积分为正而不是负，以便平行自旋排列的能量低于反平行自旋排列。其次，在磁性中，整个晶体而不是分子是结构单元，因此潜在的数学和物理复杂性甚至比最糟糕的有机分子还要大。铁磁性的量子理论成功了吗？答案在很大程度上取决于一个人是持乐观主义者的态度还是悲观主义者的态度，也就是说，是专注于理论的成功，还是寻找缺陷并强调所有严格性和收敛性的困难。类似的话也适用于价键的量子理论。如果人们要求一个理论能够预先预测哪些合金将是铁磁性的，定量地给出剩磁、磁滞等，那么就必须承认失败，尽管随着时间的推移，理论的进一步完善可能会增加其在定性预测方面的有用性。同样，如果人们期望从量子力学在化学中的当前应用中获得预先信息，比如，苯环上的哪种取代会给出特定的物理和化学性质，例如显影剂的性质等，那么注定要失望。另一方面，如果一个人对一个理论的期望是定性地理解自然为什么如此运作，并且拥有一种令人满意的信心，即如果能够进行困难的数学计算，这个机制无疑甚至能解释最复杂的现象，那么铁磁性或价键的量子理论无疑是成功的。
