> ## 一、引言
> 
> 本篇文献《铁磁性理论综述》（A Survey of the Theory of Ferromagnetism）是哈佛大学J. H. Van Vleck教授于1945年发表在《现代物理评论》（_Reviews of Modern Physics_）上的一篇经典综述。文章系统地梳理了自1907年外斯（Weiss）提出分子场理论以来，直到20世纪40年代初量子力学在铁磁性领域的核心理论发展。
> 
> 彼时，虽然量子力学已于1926年诞生，但如何从微观电子层面解释宏观铁磁现象（如高居里温度、磁化强度-温度曲线）仍是理论物理的前沿难题。Van Vleck作为该领域的奠基人之一，其撰写的这篇综述旨在为纷繁复杂的理论模型提供一个统一的批判性视角。
> 
> 本文的核心价值在于：它不仅是一篇历史回顾，更是一次深刻的理论“体检”。Van Vleck精妙地串联起**外斯唯象理论**、**海森堡（Heisenberg）交换作用模型**、**布洛赫（Bloch）自旋波理论**以及**斯通纳（Stoner）集体电子理论**，对比了“定域电子”与“巡游电子”两大范式的优劣，并指出了领域内尚未解决的根本性矛盾，例如为何理论与实验的线性关系出奇地好。这篇文献是理解固体物理学和磁性理论发展史不可或缺的基石。
> 
> ## 二、论文结构与逻辑概览
> 
> 论文遵循一条从“唯象”到“微观”，从“简单”到“复杂”，最终回归“批判”的逻辑主线。其核心论证链条可概括为以下五个阶段：
> 
> 1.  **提出问题：分子场之“谜”**：首先确立外斯分子场理论在描述铁磁现象上的巨大成功，随即尖锐地指出其根本缺陷——无法从经典物理（如偶极子相互作用）解释分子场的巨大来源。
> 2.  **微观机制的确立：海森堡模型**：引入量子力学中的**交换作用**（Exchange Interaction），阐明其为分子场的微观起源。围绕该模型，文章详细展示了不同数学近似方法（如高斯分布、贝特-佩尔斯-外斯方法、自旋波）在计算磁化率和居里温度上的尝试与困境。
> 3.  **竞争性范式的引入：斯通纳模型**：提出另一种极限情况——**集体电子铁磁性**（Collective Electron Ferromagnetism），即电子并非定域在原子，而是在整个晶体中巡游。文章详细比较了该模型与海森堡模型在解释实验现象（如非整数玻尔磁子数、比热容、磁化率-温度曲线）上的异同。
> 4.  **理论的推广与边界**：探讨交换积分符号为负时的情况，将理论框架拓展至**反铁磁性**（Anti-ferromagnetism）和**弱顺磁性**（Feeble Paramagnetism），展示了理论的统一性和解释力。
> 5.  **批判性总结与展望**：最后，Van Vleck将铁磁性理论与化学价键理论进行类比，指出两者都面临“中间态”难以计算的困境，并以一种辩证的态度评价了理论的成功与失败。
> 
> ## 三、所有图表深度解析
> 
> 论文包含6个核心图表，用于直观展示理论预测与实验结果的对比。
> 
> #### 图1：居里点以上磁化率倒数与温度的关系
> 
> _（原文描述：The reciprocal of the susceptibility as a function of temperature above the Curie point.）_
> 
> -   **内容**：展示铁（Fe）和镍（Ni）等材料的**磁化率倒数**（1/χ，纵轴）随**温度**（T，横轴，单位：°C）的变化曲线。
> -   **核心发现**：曲线在高温段接近线性，这与外斯理论预测的直线关系相符。铁在900°C左右因多晶型转变点出现不连续性，这被特别标注出来。
> -   **理论意义**：证实了外斯定律`χ = C/(T - T_c)`的有效性，为“顺磁居里点”（Paramagnetic Curie Point）`T_c`的测定提供了方法。
> 
> #### 图2：居里点附近的放大示意图
> 
> _（原文描述：Enlargement of Fig. 1 near the Curie point for nickel...）_
> 
> -   **内容**：对图1中镍的居里点附近区域进行放大，示意性地展示了理论预期与实验的偏差。
> -   **核心概念区分**：
>     -   **顺磁居里点**（`T_c`）：从高温段线性外推得到`1/χ = 0`的温度。
>     -   **铁磁居里点**（`T_c'`）：实际观测到铁磁性消失的温度。
>     -   通常`T_c` > `T_c'`，两者之差（如镍中约为20 K）代表了分子场理论的“二级效应”或涨落效应，是理论需要精修的证明。
> 
> #### 图3：饱和磁化强度随温度的变化（约化单位）
> 
> _（原文描述：Saturation intensity of magnetization as a function of temperature, in reduced units.）_
> 
> -   **坐标系**：纵轴为**约化磁化强度**（`i = M / Nμ`，无量纲），横轴为**约化温度**（`τ = T / T_c`，无量纲）。
> -   **曲线对比**：
>     -   `S = ∞`曲线：基于经典朗之万函数的外斯理论预测，是一条“普适”曲线。
>     -   `S = 1/2`曲线：基于量子力学布里渊函数的理论预测，对应自旋量子数为1/2。
>     -   实验数据点（镍、钴）：更接近`S = 1/2`的量子曲线，而非经典曲线。
> -   **结论**：强有力地证明了量子力学（自旋概念）在描述铁磁性上的优越性，被称为“对应态定律”。
> 
> #### 图4：斯通纳理论中约化磁化强度与磁化率倒数随约化温度的变化
> 
> _（原文描述：The left side... gives the saturation intensity... as a function of the reduced temperature... The right side... is proportional to the reciprocal of the susceptibility.）_
> 
> -   **结构**：分为左右两部分，横轴均为**约化温度**（`T / T_c`）。
>     -   **左侧（低于居里点）**：纵轴为**约化饱和磁化强度**（`I / I₀`）。不同曲线对应不同参数`kθ'/ε₀`，该参数正比于**交换积分**与**费米能**（电子动能导致的能带展宽）之比。
>     -   **右侧（高于居里点）**：纵轴正比于**磁化率倒数**（`1/χ`）。
> -   **关键特征**：当`kθ'/ε₀ = ∞`（即交换作用远大于能带展宽）时，曲线与图3中`S=1/2`的曲线完全一致。当此参数减小，曲线形状发生改变，左侧饱和磁化强度`I₀`可能小于1，右侧`1/χ`曲线出现弯曲。
> 
> #### 图5：绝对零度时饱和磁化强度与交换积分/费米能比的关系
> 
> _（原文描述：This figure shows that for certain values of the exchange integral the saturation intensity of magnetization at the absolute zero is less than that corresponding to complete parallelism...）_
> 
> -   **坐标系**：纵轴为**约化饱和磁矩**（`I₀`，无量纲），横轴为参数`kθ'/ε₀`（无量纲）。
> -   **核心发现**：当`kθ'/ε₀`在区间(2/3, 2)时，`I₀`的值介于0和1之间。这意味着即使在绝对零度，电子自旋也未完全平行排列，导致饱和磁矩为非整数玻尔磁子数，这与实验事实相符。这是斯通纳模型超越简单海森堡模型的独特优势之一。
> 
> #### 图6：反铁磁性材料中磁化率与约化温度的关系
> 
> _（原文描述：The susceptibility χ(T) as a function of the reduced temperature in an anti-ferromagnetic material.）_
> 
> -   **坐标系**：纵轴为**磁化率**（χ(T)，但被归一化处理，使得在居里点处为1），横轴为**约化温度**（`T / T_c`）。
> -   **曲线特征**：实线为理论曲线，虚线为MnO的实验数据。随温度升高，磁化率先上升，在居里点（**奈尔温度**）达到一个尖锐的最大值，然后下降。
> -   **物理机制**：在低温下，晶格内A、B子晶格的自旋反平行排列（**交错分子场**），外部磁场难以改变其排列，故磁化率低。温度升高，热扰动破坏了这种反平行序，磁化率升高，直至在居里点达到最大。超过居里点，热运动主导，规律服从顺磁行为。
> 
> ## 四、正文核心内容剖析
> 
> #### 4.1 研究背景：从经典到量子的跨越
> 
> 文章开篇即确立了外斯分子场理论的核心地位。该理论用一个简单的内场`H + qM`替代外场，成功解释了居里点的存在和磁化率的高温行为。但Van Vleck立刻点明其“巨大的谜团”：经典磁偶极子相互作用能（公式6）太弱，且具有强烈的各向异性，完全无法解释强度高达`10³`量级的分子场以及观测到的近似各向同性。这为量子力学的登场铺设了完美的舞台。
> 
> #### 4.2 方法模型：两大范式的博弈
> 
> 论文的核心是详细剖析了两大理论范式及其各种近似方法。
> 
> **范式一：海森堡-海特勒-伦敦（Heisenberg-Heitler-London）定域模型**
> 
> -   **核心思想**：磁性电子（如3d电子）被束缚在各自的原子周围，不参与导电。铁磁性来源于原子间纯粹的**交换作用**（公式8：`U_ij = -2J_ij S_i · S_j`），该作用通过**交换积分**`J`来量化。当`J > 0`时，自旋平行排列能量最低——产生铁磁性。
> -   **计算方法与挑战**：
>     1.  **第一性近似（Stoner推导）**：用平均值代替瞬时值，直接导出外斯分子场，得到`T_c = 2zJS(S+1)/3k`。此近似过于粗糙，甚至预测一维链状结构也能产生铁磁性，与事实不符。
>     2.  **高斯分布近似（海森堡）**：考虑能量态分布，但结果更差，导致`1/χ`-T曲线严重弯曲，且对铁磁性产生的条件过于苛刻。
>     3.  **级数展开法（Opechowski）**：将`1/χ`展开为`1/T`的级数（公式16），但级数收敛性极差，甚至出现第三、四阶修正导致面心立方晶格也不具有铁磁性的荒谬结果，此法被判定为“徒劳”。
>     4.  **贝特-佩尔斯-外斯方法（Bethe-Peierls-Weiss）**：这是文章最为推崇的“当时最佳”方法。它将一个中心原子及其最近邻原子作为一个集团，集团内精确求解量子力学问题，集团外的影响用一个自洽的“有效场”`H_e`代替。该方法成功区分了配位数（`z`）相同但结构不同的晶格（如简单立方有铁磁性，而平面六角晶格没有），并给出了闭合形式的解，而非不可靠的级数。
>     5.  **布洛赫自旋波方法（Bloch Spin Waves）**：适用于极低温（`T << T_c`）区域。它将少数翻转的自旋视为在晶格中传播的波。该方法预测低温下磁化强度的变化遵循`M = Nμ[1 - A(kT/J)^(3/2)]`（公式20），即`T^(3/2)`定律，这与实验吻合。
> 
> **范式二：斯通纳（Stoner）集体电子（巡游电子）模型**
> 
> -   **核心思想**：3d电子是巡游的，在整个晶体中自由移动，形成能带。铁磁性源于能带在交换作用下的自发**自旋劈裂**。
> -   **计算方法**：将费米-狄拉克统计与分子场思想结合。系统的特征函数（公式21）包含了对自旋向上和向下两个子能带的积分。模型的关键参数是`kθ'/ε₀`，即交换能与费米能（能带宽度）之比。
> -   **独特优势**：
>     -   自然解释了非整数玻尔磁子数（图5）。
>     -   预言了电子比热容中与温度成正比的“过剩比热容”项。
>     -   磁化率-温度曲线（图4）在高温下也近似线性，但存在弯曲。
> 
> #### 4.3 实验结果与讨论：一场旷日持久的“平局”
> 
> Van Vleck并未武断地判定哪种模型更优，而是进行了一场极为细致的“比较审判”。
> 
> -   **绝对零度磁化强度**：斯通纳模型能解释非整数玻尔磁子数，但定域模型通过引入`d⁹`和`d¹⁰`两种组态的混合也能解释（如镍）。
> -   **低温磁化行为**：海森堡-布洛赫的`T^(3/2)`定律在某些合金中优于斯通纳的`T²`定律。
> -   **高温磁化率**：两个模型都预测了`1/χ`-T曲线的近似线性关系，但也都存在无法解释的曲率问题。特别是，实验观测到的线性度出奇地好，这在理论上反而是一个“谜”，因为各种修正（如分子场涨落、自旋-轨道耦合耦合）都倾向于引入更多弯曲。
> -   **关键案例——钆（Gadolinium）**：作为4f电子磁性材料，其电子高度定域，排除了巡游效应。Trombe的实验发现其`1/χ`-T曲线线性度极好，且居里常数与海森堡第一性近似的理论值吻合。这与镍、铁形成对比，突显了问题的复杂性。
> 
> ## 五、结论与贡献
> 
> Van Vleck的结论是审慎而辩证的。
> 
> 1.  **核心机制确立**：量子力学的**交换作用**是理解铁磁性的唯一钥匙，成功驱散了外斯分子场的“最大迷雾”。
> 2.  **理论框架统一**：文章将铁磁性（`J > 0`）、反铁磁性（`J < 0`）和弱顺磁性统一在同一个交换耦合的理论框架下，并给出了清晰的物理图像，特别是通过“交错子晶格”模型解释了反铁磁性的特征磁化率最大值（图6）。
> 3.  **范式互补性揭示**：明确指出了定域（海森堡）与巡游（斯通纳）模型分别是两个极端的理想化近似，真实情况居于两者之间。两者在许多定性结论上惊人地一致，这增强了理论的可信度。
> 4.  **方法论的重大贡献**：系统性地评估了当时所有主流计算方法，尤其是高度评价了贝特-佩尔斯-外斯方法在解决级数收敛问题上的突破，以及自旋波理论在低温领域的成功。
> 5.  **提出深刻悖论**：揭示了“理论越精修，与实验的简单线性关系反而越远”这一根本性矛盾，为后续研究指明了方向。
> 
> ## 六、未来发展方向展望
> 
> 文章在结尾处暗示了未来研究的几个关键路径：
> 
> 1.  **超越极端模型**：寻找能够处理“中间态”的数学方法，即同时考虑电子的定域关联能和巡游动能，这是理论发展的必然方向。
> 2.  **解决收敛性悖论**：开发更强大的数学工具，以非微扰的方式处理海森堡模型的统计力学问题，搞清楚为何高阶近似会导致更差的结果，以及如何恢复与实验一致的简单行为。
> 3.  **定量化探索**：从定性解释走向定量预测，尤其是对特定材料的居里温度、磁化率曲线、磁各向异性能等进行精确计算。
> 4.  **关联能的计算**：在斯通纳模型中，**关联效应**（Correlation Effect，即电子间因库仑排斥而相互规避，Wigner和Seitz曾强调其重要性）被完全忽略。如何将其纳入计算是一个重大挑战。
> 5.  **自旋-轨道耦合**：文章提及磁各向异性主要源于自旋-轨道耦合，但对这一机制的理论处理在当时尚不成熟，是未来需要深入研究的领域。
> 
> ## 七、学术思考与批判性分析
> 
> 1.  **“对应态定律”的普适性争议**：Van Vleck强调外斯理论导出的`I/I₀ - T/T_c`普适曲线（图3）是“定性正确，定量错误”。这是否暗示，在铁磁相变临界点附近，存在比平均场理论更深刻的普适类规律？尽管当时尚未提出，但这正是后来肯尼斯·威尔逊（Kenneth Wilson）**重正化群理论**（Renormalization Group）要解决的相变普适性问题。Van Vleck的观察为这一革命性理论提供了早期的实验-理论矛盾证据。
>     
> 2.  **对“失败”的重新定义**：文章对理论成功与否的评价标准极具启发性。Van Vleck认为，理论的价值不在于精确预测特定材料的居里温度，而在于提供一个“如果数学上可行，便能解释一切”的机制框架。这反映了在最复杂的多体问题面前，理论物理学的核心目标有时是寻求“定性理解”和“概念框架”，而非工程学意义上的精确计算。
>

> 2.  **革命性阐释**：深入剖析了量子力学，特别是海森堡的**交换作用**模型，如何为巨大的分子场提供了合理的微观解释。
> 3.  **方法论比较**：详细对比了海森堡（束缚电子）和斯托纳（巡游电子）两种主流理论模型的优劣，展示了不同物理图像下的计算方法和结果。
> 4.  **概念性拓展**：将理论框架拓展至反铁磁性和弱顺磁性，描绘了磁性现象的完整光谱。

> ## 三、所有图表深度解析
> 
> 本文包含6幅图表，是理解理论预测与实验对比的关键。
> 
> ### 图1：居里点以上的磁化率倒数与温度关系
> 
> -   **核心内容**：展示了铁磁性物质（如Fe、Ni）在居里温度（`T_c`）以上时，**磁化率倒数 `1/\chi`** 随温度的变化。
> -   **单位/量纲**：纵轴为 `1/\chi`（无量纲或以 `emu/cm³` 的倒数表示），横轴为温度（单位：°C）。
> -   **现象关联**：完美验证了外斯理论的预言——**居里-外斯定律**，即 `1/\chi \propto (T - T_c)`。这是一条直线，其与横轴的交点即为顺磁居里点 `T_p`。
> -   **理论框架**：这是外斯分子场理论最成功的定量预测之一。
> 
> ### 图2：镍在图1中居里点附近的放大示意图
> 
> -   **核心内容**：放大了图1中镍在居里点附近的区域，以展示一个关键细节。
> -   **现象关联**：揭示了**顺磁居里点 `T_p`**（高温段直线外推值）与**铁磁居里点 `T_f`**（`1/\chi` 实际归零温度）之间的差异。`T_p` 略高于 `T_f`。
> -   **理论框架**：这个差异（约20K）是原始外斯理论无法解释的“二级效应”，它暗示了分子场并非恒定，而是存在涨落。这是后来更精确理论（如贝特-佩尔斯-外斯方法）需要解决的问题。
> 
> ### 图3：饱和磁化强度随温度的变化（对应态定律）
> 
> -   **核心内容**：展示了在居里温度以下，**约化饱和磁化强度 `I = M(T)/M(0)`** 与 **约化温度 `\tau = T/T_c`** 的普遍关系。
> -   **单位/量纲**：纵轴为无量纲比值 `I`，横轴为无量纲比值 `\tau`。
> -   **现象关联**：实验数据点（Ni, Co）与理论曲线进行对比。经典理论曲线（`S=\infty`）定性地符合实验趋势，但定量上，量子曲线（`S=1/2`）与实验符合得更好，尤其对镍而言。
> -   **理论框架**：这是外斯理论的**对应态定律**，即所有物质在此约化坐标下应遵循同一曲线。它将宏观描述的普适性与微观量子数（`S`）联系起来，是理论的一大进步。
> 
> ### 图4：斯托纳集体电子理论中的磁化强度和磁化率
> 
> -   **核心内容**：此图一分为二，左侧显示低于 `T_c` 时的约化饱和磁化强度 `I`，右侧显示高于 `T_c` 时的约化磁化率倒数。不同曲线对应不同的参数 `k\theta'/\epsilon_0`。
> -   **单位/量纲**：左侧纵轴为 `I`（无量纲），右侧纵轴为约化磁化率倒数（无量纲），横轴为约化温度 `T/\theta`。
> -   **现象关联**：参数 `k\theta'/\epsilon_0` 代表**交换能**与**能带宽度（费米能）** 之比。当此比值趋于无穷（能带极窄），结果回归经典外斯理论（`S=1/2`）。比值越小，磁化强度越低，`1/\chi` 曲线弯曲越明显。
> -   **理论框架**：这是斯托纳**巡游电子模型**的核心成果。它表明，即使交换作用有利于铁磁性，如果电子能带太宽，铁磁性仍可能被抑制。
> 
> ### 图5：绝对零度时的饱和磁矩与交换/费米能量比
> 
> -   **核心内容**：展示了在绝对零度下，饱和磁矩 `I_0` 与参数 `\epsilon_0/k\theta'` 的关系。
> -   **单位/量纲**：纵轴为 `I_0`（无量纲，以玻尔磁子为单位），横轴为 `\epsilon_0/k\theta'`（无量纲）。
> -   **现象关联**：当 `k\theta'/\epsilon_0 < 2/3` 时，`I_0` 小于1，表明即使在0K，自旋也未完全平行排列。这解释了为什么像镍这样的材料的饱和磁矩是**非整数玻尔磁子数**（0.6），而非整数。
> -   **理论框架**：这是斯托纳模型的一大胜利，它自然地从能带填充的角度解释了非整数磁矩，无需像海森堡模型那样引入复杂的电子转移假设。
> 
> ### 图6：反铁磁性材料的磁化率与温度关系
> 
> -   **核心内容**：展示了**反铁磁性**材料（如MnO）的磁化率 `\chi(T)` 随约化温度 `T/T_c` 的变化。
> -   **单位/量纲**：纵轴为磁化率 `\chi(T)`（已归一化），横轴为约化温度 `T/T_c`（无量纲）。
> -   **现象关联**：磁化率在临界温度 `T_c`（现在称为**奈尔温度 `T_N`**）达到一个宽峰，之后随温度降低而下降。实线为理论曲线，虚线为不同场强下的实验数据。
> -   **理论框架**：这是对负交换积分（`J<0`）的完美诠释。相邻自旋倾向于反平行排列，形成交错磁格子。高温下热运动破坏此有序性，磁化率遵循修正的居里-外斯定律 `\chi \propto 1/(T+|\theta|)`；低温下，强大的反平行内场使外界难以改变其磁化状态，导致磁化率下降。

> ## 四、正文核心内容剖析
> 
> ### 4.1 研究背景与问题起源：外斯分子场的成功与困境
> 
> 朗之万的顺磁性理论是故事起点，它认为原子磁矩在磁场中遵循玻尔兹曼分布，得到磁化强度公式 `M = N\mu L(\mu H/kT)`。外斯的天才在于，他提出在铁磁体内部，存在一个与磁化强度 `M` 成正比的巨大“**分子场**” `qM`，因此作用于原子磁矩的实际场是 `H + qM`。这一简单假设带来了巨大成功：
> 
> -   **推导出居里-外斯定律**：`\chi = C/(T - T_c)`, 其中 `T_c = N\mu^2q/3k`，完美解释了图1的线性关系。
> -   **建立了对应态定律**：预言了图3所示的约化饱和磁化强度对约化温度的普适函数关系。
> 
> 然而，分子场的起源是巨大的谜团。经典的**磁偶极子相互作用**能量太弱，计算出的 `q` 值仅为 `4\pi`，而实际需要约 `10^4`。此外，这种相互作用是各向异性的，与实验观察到的各向同性相悖。这为量子力学的登场埋下了伏笔。
> 
> ### 4.2 核心方法与模型：量子力学的革命性解释
> 
> #### 4.2.1 海森堡模型：交换作用的定域电子图像
> 
> 量子力学提供了两把钥匙：**电子自旋**（解释了`g`因子接近2）和**交换作用**（解释了巨大的分子场）。海森堡揭示了交换作用是库仑相互作用与泡利不相容原理共同作用的结果。其有效势能可以写成各向同性的形式： `U_{ij} = -2J_{ij} \mathbf{S}_i \cdot \mathbf{S}_j` 其中，**交换积分 `J`** 是核心。当 `J > 0` 时，自旋平行排列能量最低，为铁磁性提供了驱动力。这个“自旋-自旋”耦合纯粹是静电起源的，其能量尺度远大于磁相互作用，自然地解释了分子场 `q` 的巨大数值和各向同性。
> 
> #### 4.2.2 斯托纳模型：集体电子的巡游电子图像
> 
> 与海森堡的电子定域于原子模型相反，斯托纳将铁磁性视为**能带效应**。`d` 电子在晶格中自由巡游，形成能带。交换作用通过一个等效的分子场引入，导致自旋向上和自旋向下的能带发生劈裂。系统的磁化强度由这两个子能带的填充差异决定。该模型用一个参数 `k\theta'/\epsilon_0`（交换能/能带宽度）来控制行为。当能带很窄（`\epsilon_0` 很小，即电子高度定域），结果回归海森堡模型；当能带较宽，铁磁性可能被抑制，并能自然得到非整数玻尔磁子数。
>

> ### 4.4 理论拓展：反铁磁性与弱顺磁性
> 
> 范弗莱克指出，交换积分 `J` 并非总是正值。当 `J < 0` 时，相邻自旋倾向反平行排列。在特定晶格（可分为两个子晶格）中，这导致**反铁磁性**。其磁化率在奈尔温度 `T_N` 处出现最大值，低于 `T_N` 时急剧下降，完美解释了图6。若 `J` 为负且绝对值很大，或电子能带效应显著，材料的磁性就被极大抑制，表现为**弱顺磁性**，其磁化率很小且几乎与温度无关。这解释了为什么大多数物质并非强磁性。

> ## 五、结论与贡献
> 
> ### 5.1 核心发现
> 
> 1.  **成功解释了分子场的起源**：量子力学的**交换作用**是巨大分子场的微观来源，其强度足以克服热运动，导致自旋平行排列。
> 2.  **建立了两种互补的理论范式**：**海森堡-海特勒-伦敦模型**（定域电子）和**斯托纳模型**（巡游电子）从不同极限近似出发，共同描绘了铁磁性的物理图像。
> 3.  **统一了磁现象的理论描述**：通过交换积分 `J` 的正负和大小，将**铁磁性**（`J>0`）、**反铁磁性**（`J<0`，特定晶格）和**弱顺磁性**纳入统一框架。
> 
> ### 5.2 理论贡献与学科影响
> 
> 1.  **概念革新**：将铁磁性研究从宏观唯象学推进到微观量子力学层面，确立了交换作用的核心地位。
> 2.  **方法论贡献**：详细介绍并比较了多种理论计算方法，包括海森堡的级数展开法、贝特-佩尔斯-外斯的自洽场方法、布洛赫的自旋波方法以及斯托纳的能带论方法，为后世研究提供了丰富的工具箱。
> 3.  **哲学启示**：范弗莱克精辟地指出，铁磁性理论与化学价键理论异曲同工，都是真实物理介于“完全定域”和“完全巡游”两个极端之间。两个模型的成功共存，反而增强了我们对中间情况的信心。
>

> ## 六、未来发展方向展望
> 
> 范弗莱克在文中已指出数个亟待解决的问题，预示着未来的研究方向：
> 
> 1.  **中间耦合区域的精确处理**：如何严谨地处理电子既非完全定域也非完全自由的中间情况，仍是理论上的巨大挑战。这需要发展超越海森堡和斯托纳模型的新方法。
> 2.  **磁各向异性的量子理论**：文章承认，交换作用本身是各向同性的。解释磁各向异性（易磁化轴）需依赖**自旋-轨道耦合**，但其定量计算在1945年仍处于起步阶段，未来需要更深入的研究。
> 3.  **磁畴理论的定量化**：文中明确提到“磁畴理论”是描述磁化过程、磁滞回线和剩磁等现象的关键，但当时“定性多于定量”。发展更精确的磁畴理论，以及理解其与微观交换作用的联系，是未来的重要方向。
> 4.  **更高阶近似的收敛性问题**：海森堡模型的级数展开法（式16）收敛性极差，甚至在第四阶近似下得出荒谬结果。如何找到一种系统且收敛的计算方案，是理论物理学家面临的巨大挑战。

> # INSUFFICIENCY OF THE HEITLER-LONDON MODEL — STONER'S METHOD
> 
> 海特勒-伦敦模型的不足——斯托纳的方法 **Stoner's Method**: 斯托纳方法，一种基于能带理论的集体电子铁磁性模型。
> 
> It is to be emphasized that all the calculations with the Heisenberg theory which we have discussed in the preceding pages are based on the Heitler-London model. The latter represents a completely non-polar approximation or, in other words, it supposes that the electrons responsible for ferromagnetism always remain on the same atom and do not participate in electrical conduction. This is an idealization never completely realized in fact. Another limiting case is furnished by the model of Sommerfeld, Wigner, Seitz, and others analogous to that of Hund and Mulliken in the theory of valence. In this model of "itinerant electrons, " one supposes that the 3d electrons circulate independently and freely from one atom to another. The resulting momentary shortage or surplus of charge on any particular atom makes the crystal instantaneously polar. Theoretical calculations from this point of view have been made by Bloch, by Slater and especially by Stoner. Evidently, the truth is somewhere between the Heitler-London model and that of itinerant electrons. The actual intermediate case is too difficult to calculate. Hence Stoner's calculations fulfill a very useful role in rounding out the theory, for if we have available the predictions of theory for the approximation of completely free as well as completely bound electrons then we can see qualitatively by interpolation what is to be expected between the two limiting cases. When, as fortunately often proves true, the results are quite similar in the two extremes, we can be reasonably confident that the behavior is not very different in the intermediate region. The calculations of Stoner are based on a welldefined, clear-cut model, and so have a certain characteristic elegance. It is assumed that the electron energy levels are attributes of the whole crystal rather than the individual atom, and can be handled by the Fermi-Dirac statistics. A molecular field is used to represent the exchange interaction, which is hence treated in a somewhat phenomenological fashion, and no more completely or rigorously than in the first approximation of the Heisenberg theory. The Stoner procedure can hence be characterised as the superposition of the Weiss molecular field on the Sommerfeld theory of electronic conduction, and depicts what is called "collective electron ferromagnetism. > # COMPARATIVE MERITS OF THE HEISENBERG AND STONER THEORIES
> 
> 海森堡理论与斯托纳理论的比较
> 
> One is apt to wonder whether from the agreement with experiment or other considerations one can deduce whether the non-polar Heisenberg model or the polar Stoner one, comes closest to reality. In our opinion, it is impossible to say anything very definite on this subject. One immediately recognizes that there are a good many features common to both theories. For example, both give a reasonable behavior of the saturation below the Curie point (item (2) above). The mode of approach to the magnetization at the absolute zero is, however, represented somewhat better by the Heisenberg model (cf. item (5)). At first thought, it may seem that a non-polar theory is incapable of offering any explanation of the excess specific heat, or of the fact that the magnetization at the absolute zero is not an integral number of Bohr magnetons per atom (items (1) and (6)). This is no longer true if one uses a slight variant of the Heisenberg theory in which not all atoms have a free spin. As a typical case, let us consider nickel. Here the usual Heisenberg model with one spin per atom is to be identified with a shortage rather than surplus of one electron compared with a closed shell, in other words with the configuration d⁸ rather than d⁹. Now, all told, there are 10 electrons per atom in nickel in addition to the electrons in the interior part 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶ of the atom which are so tightly bound that they can be omitted from consideration. These 10 electrons have their choice of being housed in 4s or 3d states. The neutral Ni atom has the configuration 4s²3d⁸, indicating that lower energy is obtained by occupancy of the two 4s places than by filling out the 3d shell to the completed group 3d¹⁰. In the solid however, the 4s wave functions of adjacent atoms will overlap considerably, and as a result the totality of 4s states for the crystal will be spread into a quite wide band, which, is presumably broad compared with the 3d band. As 4s² configurations are generally not ferromagnetic, the spins will be paired in the portion of the 4s band which is occupied, and hence will not contribute to the magnetism. Probably the 4s band is so wide that the upper portion of it is higher than the 3d band, which may in first approximation be treated as infinitely narrow in comparison. If we assume that 70 percent of the 4s band is above the 3d band, then the most stable configuration will be that in which on the average 0.6 of an electron per atom is in a 4s state, and 9.4 electrons in 3d, so that symbolically the configuration is 4s⁰·⁶3d⁹·⁴. The fact that the saturation magnetization at T = 0 is .6 Bohr magneton per atom is then explained without the need of invoking the Stoner mechanism in the intermediate case shown in Fig. 5. How about the excess specific heat with this picture? The energy is undoubtedly different when two 3d⁹ atoms are adjacent than when a 3d⁹ is next to a 3d¹⁰. There will hence be a specific heat associated with the redistribution of the 3d⁹ and 3d¹⁰ states among the different lattice sites when the temperature is varied. Sixty percent of the total number of atoms are always to be kept in the configuration 3d⁹, and forty percent in 3d¹⁰, but there are an enormous variety of ways in which the two varieties of atoms can be peppered about in the crystal. This effect is rather hard to treat in a quantitative way, although some preliminary calculations have been made by H. Hurwitz. It is not known yet, for instance, whether the resulting specific heat is linear in the temperature, or whether the agreement on the order of magnitude of the parameters as determined by the various criteria is as good as discussed in item (7) for the Stoner theory. Still, Hurwitz shows that it is probable that the specific heat is not vastly different in order of magnitude from that furnished by the Stoner collective electron mechanism. Incidently if we had a pure Heisenberg model with one free spin per atom, there would still be some specific heat, due to the spectrum of energy states for the exchange energy of the crystal, or in other words due to the fluctuations in the molecular field. At low temperatures this specific heat can be treated by the Bloch spin-wave method. It is, however, proportional to T^(3/2) and so not large enough to account for the observed excess specific heat, which is linear in T. Our suggestion of a lattice built on 3d⁹ and 3d¹⁰ atoms is in a certain sense a compromise between the simple Heisenberg picture using only one type of atom and the Stoner model of freely migrating electrons. The latter neglects the interplay between the various electrons, so that it is possible for large surpluses or deficits of charge to pile up on a given atom, which can hence be regarded as momentarily existing in configurations such as 3d⁷, 3d⁸, 3d⁹, etc. In other words the Stoner theory neglects what Wigner and Seitz call the correlation energy. In our opinion the correlation effect is a rather large one, as the indications are that the spread in 3d levels due to inter-atomic overlap is smaller than the higher ionization potentials for removal of several electrons which would have to be overcome in order for the electrons to migrate freely through the crystal without much regard to one another. In other words, our generalization of the Heisenberg-Heitler-London model does not involve the high degree of instantaneous polarity found in the unmodified collective electron viewpoint, and so perhaps comes closer to the truth. In the last analysis, it is quibbling to try and say which idea is right, since the addition of appropriate correction terms, viz. the states of higher ionization in the former and correlation effects in the latter, ultimately merge the two together. Incidentally, there is, as far as we can see, no reason why in the Stoner model the number of 3d electrons per atom, or rather number of holes in the 3d shell per atom, need be taken as unity. The unit value assumed by Stoner applies only if the 4s shell is half filled, so that the average configuration is 4s¹3d⁹. This will be the case if the 4s band splitting is rather symmetrical, so that about half of the 4s band is above, and half below the 3d band. However, there is no reason why this should necessarily be true. With two undetermined parameters, viz. the total number of d electrons and the ratio kθ'/ε0, the extended Stoner theory would have so much leeway in the way of undetermined constants that it would be hard to tell whether any agreement obtained with experiment would be significant or fortuitous. The fact that the original Heisenberg theory has no undetermined constants when reduced units are used must be regarded as both an element of strength and weakness in this theory — strength in that any agreement achieved with experiment is not the result of an ad hoc choice of constants, but weakness in that not enough flexibility is included to allow for the complicated structure of a real solid. > It still remains to compare the Heisenberg and Stoner theories in their predictions regarding the reciprocal of the susceptibility as a function of temperature above the Curie point (items (3) and (4) of our preceding discussion of Stoner's work). We believe it is futile to try and decide between the two theories on this ground. We have seen that Opechowski's work shows that the series in (16) converge so badly that it is silly to correlate with the first two terms of (16) the slope of a straight line which happens to represent fairly well the theoretical dependence of 1/x on T over most of the range of T accessible to observation. Thus even in the Heisenberg theory one should not expect agreement between the magneton numbers deduced from saturation at T = 0 and from use of the first two terms of (16). More specifically, the calculations of Weiss with the Bethe-Peierls method indicate that above the Curie point, except near the latter, the graph of 1/x against T does not deviate greatly from a straight line. On the whole, with any point of view, it is easier to understand the existence of curvature in the relation between 1/x and T, or the discrepancy between the paramagnetic and ferromagnetic Curie points, than it is why the relation is as nearly linear as it is empirically. Namely, with any type of theory one always neglects certain corrections or perturbations the inclusion of which will presumably introduce more curvature. The observed linearity would seem to indicate that there is a fortuitous compensation of the various effects, and that the slope does not correspond to the value obtained by using the first term of some series development but rather that a straight line is simply a rough approximation to a complicated analytical relation. Among the various complications which affect the relation between 1/x and T are the following: (a) the fact that exchange coupling cannot be represented rigorously by the usual molecular field. In other words there exist fluctuations in the latter which are not covered by the conventional first approximation of the Heisenberg theory; (b) distortion due to the remains of spin-orbit coupling which persist despite quenching of most of the orbital angular momentum in a solid, and which interfere with the freedom of spin orientation; (c) in an ionic model, the migrations of electrons through the crystal; (d) in a generalized Heitler-London model, the redistribution of the configurations d⁹ and d¹⁰ among the different lattice sites. The calculations of Weiss with the Bethe-Peierls method allow in principle for (a), and those of Stoner include (c), but not (a). In unpublished work, Hurwitz has investigated qualitatively the effect of (d), having to rely mainly on examination of how the badly convergent series development (16) is affected by including (d). He finds that (d) increases the fluctuations of the molecular field which are present even with all atoms alike, and so tends usually to accentuate curvature. None of the existing calculations have considered (b) in any detail. All told, the observed amount of linearity in the 1/x —T relations must be regarded as something of a mystery. In order to have simpler conditions, it is illuminating to have experimental data on metallic gadolinium (not to be confused with the celebrated salt gadolinium sulphate, which is only paramagnetic). Such data are provided by the noteworthy experiments of Trombe. As the 4f electrons responsible for the magnetism of the rare earth group are deeply cloistered in the interior of the atom, and as the Gd+++ ion is in an S state, effects (b) and (c) are presumably wanting. Trombe finds indeed that the curvature in the relation between 1/x and T is considerably smaller for gadolinium than for nickel. Also he finds that at T=0 the saturation magneton number has, within the experimental error, exactly the right value for a Gd+++ ion, indicating that we have here to consider only atoms in a single configuration. What is especially interesting is that above the Curie point, the susceptibility is, except near Tq, well represented by using a Curie constant 35Np²/3k. This is the value corresponding to the first approximation of the Heisenberg theory, in which we terminate the development (16) with a and use S=7/2 since the Gd+++ ion is in an ⁸S state. It is clear that since the 4f electrons are deeply buried in the atom, one must use the Heisenberg rather than Stoner theory in gadolinium. However, it is not apparent why the fluctuations in the molecular field, whose effect is represented by the higher members of (16) should be so unimportant. In other words, there is still the complication (a) even if (b), (c), and (d) are absent, and we have indicated that (a) alone is enough to change the apparent value of the Curie constant over the domain in which linearity of 1/x in 1 holds. There thus appears to be something of a contradiction between iron or nickel and gadolinium. In other words the simple theory seems to work too well for the latter. > # QUESTION OF THE SIGN OF THE EXCHANGE INTEGRAL
> 
> 交换积分符号的问题
> 
> In order to have ferromagnetism, it is essential that the exchange integral defined by (10) be positive. Otherwise the exchange effects tend to destroy rather than amplify the magnetic moment, or in other words the constant of the molecular field is negative and has a demagnetizing effect. Hitherto in this article we have tacitly omitted examination of whether the exchange integral had the proper sign to produce ferromagnetism. Obviously it would be desirable if one could calculate this sign theoretically, for then one would be able to predict whether a given material might be ferromagnetic. We use the word "might" rather than "is" because the work of Bloch and Stoner shows that even with a favorable sign of the exchange integral, ferromagnetism is suppressed if the spreading of energy levels due to electron migration is too large. Unfortunately it is so difficult to calculate exchange integrals in ferromagnetic materials that this has not been done. Even for electrons belonging to free hydrogenic atoms the computation of these integrals would be laborious, and undoubtedly interatomic forces distort the d electrons so badly that conclusions based on calculations with free atoms would probably not be reliable, at least without some further analysis. There is, however, one working rule, first developed by Slater, which seems to furnish a rather convenient qualitative index as to whether ferromagnetism is possible. It states that ferromagnetism can exist only if the atomic radius is small compared to the interatomic distance. Furthermore, Sommerfeld and Bethe have shown that the Slater rule has a simple, though rather crude quantummechanical explanation, as follows. The bulk of the contribution to the integral (10) presumably comes from regions, in the six-dimensional coordinate space, which are exterior to the outermost node of the wave functions of both electrons, at least provided the azimuthal quantum number is zero or small. The contribution of the region inside the nodes is considered of minor significance by Sommerfeld and Bethe because the wave functions will not overlap sufficiently to penetrate strongly the interior portion of a neighboring atom and also to a certain extent because the volume covered by the interior regions of the atom is small. Thus, though this is perhaps the weakest part of the argument, the dominant part of the integral is considered to arise from regions for which the factor φi*(1)φj*(2)φi(2)φj(1) is positive, so that the sign behavior of the integral can be gauged by examining the sign of K. After allowing for terms that cancel out because φi, φj satisfy respectively the wave equations for free atoms i and j respectively, it is found that the significant parts of K are those corresponding to the potential energy due to the nucleus of the other atom, with allowance for screening by non-valence electrons, and to the mutual potential energy of the two electrons involved in the exchange integral. If the nuclear attractive term predominates over the inter-electronic repulsive term, the factor K will be essentially negative; otherwise it will be positive. Now if the radius of the atom is at all comparable with the interatomic distance, there will be appreciable overlapping of the wave functions, i.e., a significant factor φi*(1)φj*(2)φi(2)φj(1), at points where the interelectronic repulsion is overshadowed by the nuclear term inasmuch as a nucleus contains many electronic charges. On the other hand, when the atomic radius is very small compared with the interatomic distance, the only appreciable overlapping will be in a more or less void space midway between the two atoms, where the influence of both atomic centers is unimportant and the important energetic term arises only because of the possibility of the two electrons themselves sometimes being close together in this midway region. Hence one can see qualitatively why in order for the repulsive terms to outweigh the nuclear effects and give a positive exchange integral, one must satisfy the Slater criterion that the atomic radius be small compared to the interatomic distance. The Sommerfeld-Bethe argument, moreover, goes further and indicates why ferromagnetism is found only for d and f electrons and never for s electrons. Namely, the atomic wave functions contain a factor r^l, where l is the azimuthal quantum number. In s-states the absence of a node at the nucleus presumably makes the negative contribution to the exchange integral predominate if the atomic diameter is not completely negligible compared to the inter-atomic distance, so that there is some overlapping. Hence we can understand why s-electrons never give ferromagnetism. On the other hand, in d- or f-states the factor r^l makes the integrand small in the interior of an atom where the nuclear forces are greatest. In order for there to be ferromagnetism it is not sufficient just that the exchange integral be positive, but in addition it must be sufficiently large to predominate over any energy band structure due to electron migration (cf. Fig. 5). The factor r^l is helpful not merely because it enhances the likelihood of a positive sign, but also because it will make the integral larger once it has become positive, by accentuating the outer regions where the interelectronic repulsion is the prime effect. > # FEEBLE PARAMAGNETISM
> 
> 弱顺磁性 **Feeble paramagnetism**: 弱顺磁性，指磁化率远小于正常顺磁体，且几乎不随温度变化的顺磁性。
> 
> From the preceding discussion it is apparent that the large positive exchange integrals necessary for ferromagnetism will be realized only under rather special favorable conditions. Normally the exchange integral will be negative, and the spins will tend to set themselves anti-parallel. This is the situation ordinarily encountered for the electrons responsible for chemical bonding. In fact, the usual theories of valence, electronpairing, etc., are founded on the idea that a saturated chemical bond corresponds to the compounding of the individual atomic spins to a zero resultant molecular spin. This is just the reverse of the state of affairs necessary for ferromagnetism, since the latter demands that the configuration of lowest energy be one of high rather than compensated spin. Thus ferromagnetism may be regarded as a sort of exception to the usual valence rules. For the normal case of negative exchange integrals, the effect of exchange is demagnetizing, and may roughly be described by taking the Curie temperature Tc to be negative in the Weiss Curie formula x = C/(T - Tc), since Tc has the same sign as the exchange integral. If the exchange coupling is powerful enough so that -Tc is large compared with T, then the susceptibility will be independent of temperature, and much smaller than for a free atom, where Tc=0. Thus a mechanism is provided for understanding why many substances, far outnumbering the strongly paramagnetic, or ferromagnetic ones, have a feeble paramagnetism which is more or less independent of temperature. In a feebly paramagnetic material, the spin of an individual atom can be as large as in a highly magnetic substance, but the exchange effects are demagnetizing and tend to make the resultant spin of the entire crystal small. A typical metal of this type is aluminum, which is only weakly paramagnetic despite the fact that the normal state of the Al atom is ²P. Strictly speaking, any discussion of feeble paramagnetism is outside the scope of a report on ferromagnetism. However, weakly paramagnetic materials are so common, and the theoretical explanation of their behavior ties in so closely with that of ferromagnetics, that before closing it has seemed worth while to comment on them briefly. We should mention that there are other effects besides negative exchange coupling which enfeeble paramagnetism. In particular there is the suppression of paramagnetism due to the spreading of energy levels caused by the migration of the electrons through a solid conductor. Pauli first showed that this dispersion of translational energy levels removes most of the spin paramagnetism when there is no exchange coupling, and we have seen that Bloch and Stoner have demonstrated that this can still be true even when the exchange integrals have a sign favorable to ferromagnetism, provided they are not too large. Since the wave functions for s and p electrons usually overlap adjacent atoms considerably, the migration effects are probably sufficient, regardless of the sign of the exchange integrals, to suppress most of the paramagnetism in solids not having d or f electrons. Special mention should be made of the fact that in the nonferromagnetic elements of the transition groups, the paramagnetism, though still of what may be called the "feeble" variety, is considerably larger than usual for this category. Precisely this behavior is to be expected theoretically, since the wave functions of d electrons overlap other atoms less than do those of the s or p type, and in consequence there is a narrower band structure, with less suppression of paramagnetism. For a very interesting description of the experimental data on this subject, data on the widths of the energy bands, etc., the reader is referred particularly to the discussion in Mott and Jones' book. So far we have cited only feeble paramagnetism which arises because of nearly completing quenching of the magnetic moments of atoms which are paramagnetic when free. Sometimes distortion due to interatomic forces can originate a weak paramagnetism in materials whose isolated atoms or ions are diamagnetic. This situation is more likely to arise in an ionic salt than in a conductor, inasmuch as electrons of ions in closed shells usually migrate but little. Examples are probably furnished by Sc₂O₃, TiO₂, CeO₂, and numerous cobaltamines. 从前面的讨论可以明显看出，铁磁性所需的大正交换积分，只会在相当特殊且有利的条件下才能实现。通常情况下，交换积分将是负的，自旋将倾向于使自己排列成反平行。这是负责化学键合的电子通常遇到的情况。事实上，通常的价键理论、电子配对等，都建立在饱和化学键对应于单个原子自旋组合成零总分子自旋的思想之上。这与铁磁性所需的状况正好相反，因为后者要求最低能量构型是高自旋的，而不是补偿自旋的。因此，铁磁性可以被视为对通常价键规则的一种例外。对于负交换积分的正常情况，交换效应是退磁的，并且可以通过在外斯居里公式 x = C/(T - Tc) 中将居里温度Tc取为负值来粗略描述，因为Tc与交换积分具有相同的符号。如果交换耦合足够强，以至于 -Tc 与 T 相比较大，那么磁化率将与温度无关，并且比自由原子（Tc=0）的磁化率小得多。这样就提供了一个机制，用于理解为什么许多物质（其数量远远超过强顺磁性或铁磁性物质）具有或多或少与温度无关的弱顺磁性。在弱顺磁性材料中，单个原子的自旋可以像在强磁性物质中一样大，但交换效应是退磁的，并倾向于使整个晶体的总自旋变小。这类金属的一个典型例子是铝，它只是弱顺磁性的，尽管铝原子的正常态是²P。严格地说，任何关于弱顺磁性的讨论都超出了铁磁性报告的范围。然而，弱顺磁性材料是如此普遍，并且对其行为的理论解释与铁磁体的解释如此紧密相连，以至于在结束之前，似乎值得对其简要评论一下。我们应该提到，除了负交换耦合之外，还有其他效应会减弱顺磁性。特别是，由于电子在固体导体中迁移导致的能级展宽，会抑制顺磁性。泡利首先证明，当没有交换耦合时，这种平动能级的色散消除了大部分自旋顺磁性，而我们已经看到，布洛赫和斯托纳证明了，即使交换积分具有有利于铁磁性的符号，只要它们不是太大，这也可能仍然成立。由于s和p电子的波函数通常与相邻原子有相当大的重叠，迁移效应很可能足以抑制不含d或f电子的固体中的大部分顺磁性，无论交换积分的符号如何。应该特别提及这样一个事实，即在过渡族的非铁磁性元素中，顺磁性虽然仍属于可被称为“弱”的类型，但比此类别的通常情况要大得多。这种行为正是理论上所预期的，因为d电子的波函数与其他原子的重叠程度小于s或p型电子，因此能带结构更窄，对顺磁性的抑制更少。对于关于这个主题的实验数据、能带宽度数据等非常有趣的描述，请读者特别参考莫特和琼斯书中的讨论。到目前为止，我们只引用了弱顺磁性，这种弱顺磁性是由于本应在自由状态下具有顺磁性的原子的磁矩几乎完全猝灭而产生的。有时，原子间力引起的畸变可以在其孤立原子或离子是抗磁性的材料中产生弱顺磁性。这种情况更可能发生在离子盐中，而不是在导体中，因为闭壳层中离子的电子通常很少迁移。例子可能由Sc₂O₃, TiO₂, CeO₂和许多钴胺化合物提供。

> # ANTI-FERROMAGNETISM
> 
> 反铁磁性 **Anti-ferromagnetism**: 反铁磁性，指相邻原子或离子的磁矩反向平行排列，使得总磁矩为零的磁性状态。
> 
> In the preceding discussion we have stressed how difficult it is to know whether feeble paramagnetism is to be attributed to negative exchange integrals rather than to some of the other mechanisms which we have mentioned. There is, however, one class of materials, known as "anti-ferromagnetics" in which it is quite clear that the suppression of paramagnetism is to be identified with exchange coupling. These substances, which are not very common, have a susceptibility which passes through a maximum when the temperature is raised, as shown in Fig. 6. On the other hand, ordinary feeble paramagnetism is characterized by a susceptibility which is more or less independent of temperature, but which usually decreases monotonically and slowly as the temperature is raised. Examples of anti-ferromagnetic media include MnO, MnSe, Cr₂O₃, and CrSb. The peculiar behavior illustrated in Fig. 6 is nicely explained theoretically in the following way. Suppose that we have a crystal whose constituent atoms can be resolved into two sublattices A and B such that the nearest neighbors of the atoms of A are atoms belonging to B, and vice versa. The simple cubic and body-centered cubic lattices are both of this type. In our rudimentary derivation of the Weiss molecular field from the exchange potential (8), we assumed that on the average the spins of all atoms pointed in the same direction. This supposition is admissible if the exchange integral is positive, as then the state of lowest exchange energy is that of complete parallelism. With a negative integral, however, the exchange energy of two atoms is a minimum if their spins are antiparallel. Hence the configuration of deepest energy for the crystal as a whole is that in which the spins of sublattice A all point northward, and those of B all southward or vice versa. Consequently if a method based on mean values is used, the theory should be generalized by assuming that the mean direction of the spin in sublattice A is the opposite from that in B, apart from polarization effects due to an external applied field. With this generalized model, a "staggered" non-vanishing molecular field can exist which tends to direct the spins of A in one direction and those of B in the other. We shall omit the mathematical analysis, which is due to Neel, Bitter, and the writer. It yields the result that there is a certain critical or Curie temperature above which the spontaneous field of this character disappears. With similarly rather than oppositely directed spins for the two sublattices, no spontaneous field would have been possible at any temperature. If one treats the staggered model by a method of approximation analogous to the usual first-order one of the Heisenberg theory, then it turns out that the formula for the Curie temperature above which the staggered molecular field disappears is precisely the same as that (15) obtained for the ferromagnetic rather than anti-ferromagnetic case, except that now one must use the absolute value of the exchange integral. (Without use of the absolute value, the critical temperature would be negative, and hence meaningless. ) As the temperature is lowered, the inner field becomes stronger and stronger, and so it is harder for the field to make the spins all point in one direction when they want to be arranged alternately in opposite directions. Hence, without going through the analysis, one can see qualitatively why the susceptibility diminishes as the temperature is lowered from the Curie temperature to the absolute zero. Above the Curie temperature, on the other hand, the staggering effect is washed out, and the susceptibility decreases with increasing temperature, obeying the familiar formula C/(T+θ) (θ>0) obtainable even without introducing interlocking lattices. At the absolute zero, the inhibiting effect of the powerful internal fields on any change of alignment due to a weak external field is complete only if the latter has, apart from sense, the same direction as the former. If applied perpendicular to the alternating inner fields, the external field can still give rise to an outstanding moment by twisting slightly the orientations of the elementary magnets. Since the molecular field is presumably directed at random, the parallel and perpendicular cases, considered respectively by Bitter and Neel, should be weighted in the ratio 1:2, and it is for this reason that the susceptibility χ0 at the absolute zero for weak fields is theoretically 2/3 that χc at the Curie point. The observed values of χ0/χc, instead of being 0.67, range from 0.3 to 0.85, so that the agreement is qualitative rather than quantitative. It is especially to be emphasized that in a truly ferromagnetic medium a comparatively weak field will suffice to orient the moments of the elementary domains and make them parallel to the applied field, producing so-called saturation. On the other hand, in an anti-ferromagnetic material, even a very powerful field will produce but little rotation of the domain alignments, as the low susceptibility makes the energy of the crystal rather insensitive to external fields. One can understand qualitatively why the observations of Bizette, Squire, and Tsai on MnO shown in Fig. 6 reveal a somewhat higher susceptibility, below the Curie point, for a field of 24,000 gauss than for one of 7,000 gauss. Namely, in an anti-ferromagnetic medium a very powerful field will rotate the elementary domains into more favorable alignment, i.e., make the molecular field of more domains perpendicular to the applied field, instead of being random. In the limiting case of complete perpendicularity, the susceptibility below the Curie temperature Tc is independent of temperature and has the same value as at Tc. The rather mild dependence of susceptibility on field strength shown in Fig. 6 is to be contrasted with the behavior of a truly ferromagnetic material where a field of less than 7000 gauss would have produced complete parallelism. When the susceptibility is finite but still is somewhat influenced by field strength, one should expect some weak hysteresis phenomena, and in MnS a feeble hysteresis is indeed observed, wherein the loop is so narrow that it encloses but little area. In the preceding paragraphs we have perhaps inadvertently given the impression that feebly paramagnetic and anti-ferromagnetic media represent two distinct cases. Actually there is no sharp dividing line between the two, though a convenient criterion for anti-ferromagnetism is a maximum susceptibility at a particular temperature. Also our statement at the beginning of the discussion on feeble paramagnetism that the temperature dependence of susceptibility is of the form C/(T+ |Tc|) in the first approximation comparable with the Heisenberg theory is seen to be not really correct below the Curie point if a division into alternating sublattices is possible, for then one has a behavior of the type shown in Fig. 6. In either case, however, the variation with temperature is small, in fact less with the situation covered by Fig. 6, which gives χ0 = 2/3 χc, rather than χ0 = 1/2 χc. The fact that most weakly paramagnetic materials do not show the peculiar maximum in χ may mean that the crystal structure is not such as to permit the requisite division into sublattices, or much more likely, that the effects of electron migration are appreciable. With the Stoner collective electron mechanism, localization on individual atoms is lost, and no molecular field of consequence is possible as long as the mean exchange integral is negative, since staggering requires local order. Here we thus have a case where the results are different with the approximations of bound and itinerant electrons, and the former must be closer to the truth when a behavior such as that shown in Fig. 6 is realized. > In closing we should like to stress once more the great parallelism in many respects between the theory of ferromagnetism and the theory of valence. Both topics are readily tractable only for models corresponding to the limiting cases in which the electrons are treated respectively as tightly bound on individual atoms and as wandering freely from atom to atom without cognizance of each others' positions. The original Heisenberg theory of magnetism is the analogue of the Heitler-London-Slater-Pauling theory of valence, while the Stoner treatment resembles the Hund-Mulliken approach. In both subjects the truth is doubtless intermediate between the two extremes, and the results in the limiting cases usually do not differ too much from each other, giving one some confidence in a similar behavior in the intermediate region. In both valence and ferromagnetism, the agreement with experiment is often surprisingly good in the first approximation and in many cases spoiled by inclusion of the second or third approximation, leaving it something of a mystery why so nearly the first-order result would presumably be restored were it possible to carry through the calculations rigorously. Along with the similarity that we have noted, there are two main differences between the theory of ferromagnetism and that of the chemical bond. In the first place, the former requires a positive rather than negative exchange integral, in order that the energy be lowest for parallel rather than anti-parallel spin alignment. Secondly, in magnetism the entire crystal rather than the molecule is the structural unit, and consequently the potential mathematical and physical complexity is greater than for even the worst organic molecule. Has the quantum theory of ferromagnetism been a success? The answer depends to a large extent on whether one takes the attitude of the optimist or the pessimist, that is to say, whether one concentrates on the successes of the theory or looks for the flaws and emphasizes all the difficulties of rigor and convergence. Analogous remarks apply to the quantum theory of valence. If one demands a theory that will predict in advance just which alloys will be ferromagnetic, what quantitatively will be the remanence, hysteresis, etc., then one must admit failure, although as time progresses, greater refinement of the theory will perhaps increase its usefulness for qualitative prognostication. Similarly one is doomed to disappointment in the present applications of quantum mechanics to chemistry if one expects advance information, say, on just which substitutions in a benzene ring will give particular physical and chemical properties such as, say, that of photographic developer, etc. If, on the other hand, what one desires of a theory is a qualitative understanding of why nature works as it does, and the satisfying confidence of having a mechanism which would, no doubt, explain even the most complicated phenomena if the difficult mathematical calculations could be made, then the quantum theory of ferromagnetism or of valence is certainly a success. 最后，我们想再次强调铁磁性理论与价键理论在许多方面的极大相似性。这两个主题都只有在对应于极限情况的模型下才容易处理，在这些极限情况下，电子分别被视为紧紧地束缚在单个原子上，以及被视为在原子间自由游荡而不顾及彼此的位置。最初的海森堡磁性理论是海特勒-伦敦-斯莱特-鲍林价键理论的类比，而斯托纳的处理方法则类似于洪德-马利肯的方法。在这两个主题中，真相无疑介于两个极端之间，并且极限情况下的结果通常彼此相差不太大，这使得人们对中间区域类似的行为有了一些信心。在价键和铁磁性两者中，第一级近似与实验的一致性常常好得惊人，而在许多情况下，这种一致性被包含第二或第三级近似所破坏，留下了一个谜，即如果能够严格地完成计算，为什么几乎可以肯定会恢复第一级近似的果。除了我们已经注意到的相似性之外，铁磁性理论和化学键理论之间还有两个主要区别。首先，前者要求交换积分为正而不是负，以便平行自旋排列的能量低于反平行自旋排列。其次，在磁性中，整个晶体而不是分子是结构单元，因此潜在的数学和物理复杂性甚至比最糟糕的有机分子还要大。铁磁性的量子理论成功了吗？答案在很大程度上取决于一个人是持乐观主义者的态度还是悲观主义者的态度，也就是说，是专注于理论的成功，还是寻找缺陷并强调所有严格性和收敛性的困难。类似的话也适用于价键的量子理论。如果人们要求一个理论能够预先预测哪些合金将是铁磁性的，定量地给出剩磁、磁滞等，那么就必须承认失败，尽管随着时间的推移，理论的进一步完善可能会增加其在定性预测方面的有用性。同样，如果人们期望从量子力学在化学中的当前应用中获得预先信息，比如，苯环上的哪种取代会给出特定的物理和化学性质，例如显影剂的性质等，那么注定要失望。另一方面，如果一个人对一个理论的期望是定性地理解自然为什么如此运作，并且拥有一种令人满意的信心，即如果能够进行困难的数学计算，这个机制无疑甚至能解释最复杂的现象，那么铁磁性或价键的量子理论无疑是成功的。

> **第一段：外斯的“分子场”是个啥？**

> 1.  **背景——朗之万的理论：** 在外斯之前，有个叫朗之万的科学家，解释了一种叫“顺磁性”的现象。啥是顺磁性呢？您可以想象，有些材料的原子就像一个个微小的指南针。平时，这些“小指南针”是杂乱无章的，谁也不听谁的。但是，您一拿块大磁铁（也就是外加一个磁场）靠近它们，这些小指南针就会想跟大磁铁对齐，多少带点磁性。朗之万说，这些小指南针有多听话，遵守一个叫“玻尔兹曼分布”的统计规律，这规矩就像是一种“从众心理”，温度越高，它们越不听话，到处乱转。这就得出了一个公式，咱们叫它公式(1)和(2)。

> 1.  **海森堡的第一近似（外斯理论的再现）：** 海森堡自己也做了个近似。他假设，某个原子感受到的周围原子的作用，可以用一个平均值来代替。这个平均值，就和一个外加的分子场效果一样。这样一来，他就重新推导出了外斯理论的结果，并且把外斯那个有点玄乎的“分子场”常数q，和实实在在的交换积分J联系了起来，公式(14)和(15)。这就像给外斯理论发了张“量子力学身份证”。
>     
> 2.  **更高阶的近似和Opechowski的尝试：** 第一近似很粗糙，因为实际作用力是瞬息万变的，不是一成不变的平均值。海森堡也尝试了更精确的计算（高斯近似），但结果反而更糟糕。后来，Opechowski想用级数展开的方法（公式16），一阶一阶地算下去，希望得到更准的结果。但算到第三、第四阶，就发现这个级数收敛性很差，越算越离谱，这条路看来走不通。
>     
> 3.  **贝特-佩尔斯-外斯方法（B-P-W方法）：** 这是一个很聪明的方法，是一个叫彼得·外斯的美国年轻人（注意，不是提出分子场的法国人皮埃尔·外斯）想出来的。他的思路是：“精确处理一小团，平均处理一大片”。他把一个原子和它周围紧邻的几个原子看成一个“小团体”，团体内原子间的相互作用，用量子力学精确求解。然后，这个小团体和外面广大世界的相互作用，就用一个平均的“有效场”来代替。这个有效场的大小，通过一个自洽条件来确定：就是团体里的中心原子和外围原子，它们的平均磁矩必须相等。这个方法比之前的都好，结果也比较合理。比如，它能算出简单立方晶格有铁磁性，而平面六角晶格（虽然邻居数也是6）却没有铁磁性，这跟其他更精确的方法结论一致。
>     
> 4.  **布洛赫的自旋波方法：** 这个方法特别适合在极低温下使用。当温度接近绝对零度时，几乎所有原子的自旋都是整齐排列的，只有极少数“刺头”会翻转。布洛赫发现，这些翻转的自旋不会停留在一个原子上，而是会像波浪一样在晶体中传播，这就是“自旋波”。他用这个方法，算出了在极低温下，磁化强度随温度的变化规律是M(T) = M(0) * (1 - A*T^(3/2))，公式(20)。这个3/2次方的关系，后来被实验证实了，非常漂亮。
>     
> 
> **第四段：斯托纳的“巡游电子”模型**
> 
> 前面讲的都是海森堡模型，它假设电子是老老实实待在单个原子上的。但金属里的电子，特别是那些对导电和磁性都很重要的d电子，是会到处跑的，这叫“巡游电子”。斯托纳就从这个角度，把能带理论（索末菲理论）和外斯的分子场思想结合起来，提出了一个“集体电子铁磁性”模型。他的公式(21)非常复杂，但核心思想是：电子在固体中不再属于单个原子，而是属于整个晶体，它们的能量形成一个个“能带”。电子在能带里填充，由于交换作用，自旋向上的电子和自旋向下的电子会受到不同的“分子场”作用，导致它们能带发生劈裂，从而产生净磁矩。
> 
> 这个模型有很多有意思的结果：
> 
> -   它能解释为什么在绝对零度时，饱和磁矩不一定是整数个玻尔磁子（比如镍是0.6个）。
> -   它能解释金属中额外的“电子比热”。
> -   它和温度的关系，在居里点以上，1/x和T不再是完美的直线，这跟实验观察到的弯曲有相似之处。
> 
> **第五段：两大模型的比较**
> 
> 简单说，海森堡模型（电子局域）和斯托纳模型（电子巡游）是两个极端，现实情况应该在两者之间。两种模型各有利弊，都能解释一部分现象，也都存在不足。比如，对于低温下磁化强度随温度的关系，海森堡模型（自旋波）的 T^(3/2) 定律比斯托纳模型的 T^2 定律更符合实验。文章最后说，关于1/x和T的关系为什么在实验中几乎是一条直线，其实挺让人费解的，因为理论上各种修正都会引入弯曲，所以这可能是一种侥幸的补偿效果。
> 
> **第六段：交换积分的符号和“反铁磁性”**
> 
> 1.  **符号决定一切：** 交换积分J必须是正的，才有可能产生铁磁性（自旋平行）。如果J是负的，交换作用就会让相邻原子的自旋趋向于反平行排列，这就不是铁磁性了。
> 2.  **弱顺磁性：** 大多数材料，交换积分J是负的，或者电子巡游效应太强，抑制了磁性。它们就表现出很弱的、几乎不随温度变化的顺磁性。
> 3.  **反铁磁性：** 有一种特殊的材料，叫“反铁磁体”（比如MnO）。你可以把它的原子分成A、B两套子晶格，A上的原子自旋都想朝上，B上的都想朝下，而且互相抵消。所以宏观上，它不显磁性。但它的磁化率随温度变化有一个很特殊的峰值（图6），这个峰值对应的温度叫“奈尔温度”，是反铁磁性的特征。文章里详细解释了这种现象的成因。
> 
> **结论：** 文章最后总结说，铁磁性理论和化学键理论非常像，都面临着“电子局域”和“电子巡游”两个极端模型的困难，真实情况在这两者之间。量子理论在过去几十年里取得了巨大成功，它给了我们一个定性的、深刻的物理图像，让我们明白了铁磁性从何而来。但如果你指望它能像算命一样，精确预测哪种合金是铁磁体，它的矫顽力、剩磁是多少，那它还不够格。这就像量子化学能解释化学键的本质，但难以精确预测一个新药分子的全部性质一样。
