# DCS (4-methyl-2,5-dicyano-4'-aminostilbene)

DCS represents a highly engineered D-π-A (Donor-π-Acceptor) push-pull chromophore derived from the [[stilbene]] scaffold. Its molecular design features a dual-cyano substitution at the 2 and 5 positions of one benzene ring, which serves as a potent electron-withdrawing moiety and simultaneously extends the π-conjugation length. This specific substitution pattern is critical for enhancing both the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) and the non-linear optical response [[../papers/Huang2019solvatochromic]].

### Electronic Structure and ICT Mechanism
The optical properties of DCS are governed by a strong ICT process. Upon photoexcitation, electronic density shifts from the amino donor group (e.g., dimethylamino in derivative P1) across the stilbene π-bridge toward the dicyano-substituted acceptor ring. This transition creates a large excited-state dipole moment, rendering the emission highly sensitive to the local dielectric environment—a phenomenon known as solvatochromism. In the core study of this entity, the emission peak shifts from ~445 nm in cyclohexane to ~641 nm in DMSO, representing one of the widest solvatochromic ranges for stilbene-based systems [[../papers/Huang2019solvatochromic]].

### Two-Photon Absorption and Nonlinear Optics
DCS-based molecules exhibit exceptionally large two-photon absorption (TPA) cross-sections ($\delta$). In non-polar solvents, the $\delta_{max}$ can reach up to 6670 GM within the 800–840 nm excitation window, which coincides with the "optical window" of biological tissues [[../papers/Huang2019solvatochromic]]. The physical basis for this high efficiency lies in the synergistic effect of the ortho/meta dicyano groups, which maximize the transition dipole moment and effectively stabilize the charge-separated state.

### Multi-State Fluorescence and Phase-Locked Properties
In high-viscosity environments or restricted geometries, DCS manifests a rare "triple fluorescence" phenomenon. This involves the simultaneous existence of three distinct electronic states:
1.  **Localized Excited (LE) State (B-band)**: A state where the excitation remains largely confined to the molecular skeleton, typically dominant in rigid or high-viscosity media.
2.  **Twisted Intramolecular Charge Transfer (TICT) State (A-band)**: A state where the donor group rotates 90° relative to the π-bridge, leading to complete charge separation. This rotation is suppressed by high viscosity, making the LE/TICT intensity ratio a sensitive probe for microviscosity [[../papers/Huang2019solvatochromic]].
3.  **Exciplex State (E-band)**: A transient charge-transfer complex formed between an excited DCS molecule and a ground-state neighbor, appearing as a broad, red-shifted emission band.

### Physical Characteristics and Synthesis
Synthetically, DCS is accessible via Wittig-Horner condensation between 2,5-dicyano-benzyl phosphonate and 4-aminobenzaldehyde derivatives [[../papers/Huang2019solvatochromic]]. The resulting materials typically exist as crystalline powders with high thermal stability (melting points >200 °C). The balance between the planarity of the stilbene backbone and the rotational degrees of freedom of the donor/acceptor substituents defines the complex photophysics of the DCS entity.
