- Mechanoluminescence (ML) is the phenomenon that a solid emits photons when it is fractured or deformed [18]. This phenomenon has been observed in many kinds of solids including ionic crystals, semiconductors, metals, glasses and organic crystals [19-20]. In polycrystalline Sr3Al2O6:Eu, Dy intense ML was reported [21]. For SrAl2O4:Eu2+ it was demonstrated that ML is due to 4f7-4f65d transition of Eu2+ [22]. ML from monoclinic structure SrAl2O4 was reported and it was found that only the -SrAl2O4 phase produces strong ML [23]. These phenomenons are attracting considerable attention because they can be applied to sensing of structural damage and fracture. Although it was reported that SrAl2O4 doped with Eu2+ exhibits ML, the atomistic depiction about the effect of stress or strain on the rare-earth ion still remains unclear.
- In this paper we measured ML and Thermoluminescence (TL) intensities of Eu-doped SrAl2O4 prepared by combustion synthesis at initiating temperature of 600oC. There is no strong statement on the ML phenomenon for Eu doped SrAl2O4 system prepared by combustion synthesis to the best of our knowledge.

- Due to non-deterministic characteristics of GST, different PCM cells, even the same cell at different times, respond differently to the same programming pulse [3]. Therefore, programming PCM widely adopts iteration-based programming and verifying (P&V) scheme. Figure 3 illustrates how to write “1”. Here a RESET operation is always conducted to leave cells in similar initial states. A series of SET operations and verify (read) operations follow until the target resistance level is reached. Iterative P&V write strategy is able to precisely control the written resistance in each PCM cell such that bit error rate stays low. P&V programming leads to large PCM write latency. Recent prototypes often have more than 10× slower write latency than that of PCM read [19]. For example, Figure 4 shows that it takes 50ns and 1000ns to read and write a PCM memory line respectively.
- With the repeated heat stress applied to the phase change material, a PCM cell survives only a limited number of write cycles. Referred to as write endurance, it is a key parameter in designing PCM-based memory systems. A typical PCM cell can sustain 108 to 109 writes before it gets stuck at the SET or RESET states [2]. PCM’s write endurance is better than NAND flash (105 write cycles before failure), and worse than DRAM (1015 write cycles before failure). Each PCM cell has an optimal RESET current, i.e., the minimal current that fully reset the cell. Studies confirmed that overprogramming i.e., programming a cell with larger than its optimal RESET current, is one of the major sources that fail a PCM cell prematurely. It has been reported that a 2× increase of write energy reduces about 50× write endurance [18]. Unfortunately overprogramming is almost inevitable at chip level. With technology scaling into sub-micron regime, difficulties in manufacturing control lead to non-negligible process variations in gate length of access transistors and structure parameters of PCM cells. When writing a memory line, different cells in the line require slightly different optimal RESET currents. To reliably program all cells, the write current is set the maximum of all optimal RESET currents, which overprograms most cells and greatly shortens their lifetime. Instead of adopting one write current for the entire chip, Zhang et al. proposed current provision to choose one current per 4MB memory block [46]. Current provision helps to mitigate overprogramming and thus extend chip lifetime.
- The frequency of offchip writes is still high, which makes it unsuitable to construct pure PCM-based main memory. Instead, hybrid main memory systems are constructed using both PCM and DRAM. As shown in Figure 5, there are two possible hybrid organizations. Qureshi et al. placed a DRAM buffer between on-chip cache and PCM main memory [26], which helps to reduce the write traffic to PCM and improves the access latency of offchip misses. As in Figure 5(a), the DRAM buffer is invisible to the OS, and is managed by the memory controller. The experimental results showed that on average a small DRAM buffer (3% of PCM storage, 13% area overhead) achieves 3× speedup and 3× lifetime extension [26].

- These memristors have historically been strong in many aspects, such as scalability, fast switching speed, long retention, large ON/OFF ratio, multilevel cell operation, nondestructive reading, simple structure, stackability, low cost, CMOS compatibility and manufacturability. Scalability is one of the main reasons that memristors are so interesting. In the oxide devices, information is stored or processed as the position or movement of ionic species instead of electrons [49]. Therefore, devices can be scaled down to a few nanometers without stability issues [33] [35]. On the one hand, since atoms are much heavier than electrons and do not tunnel through barriers, the device state is fairly stable at room temperature (RT) with a retention of years, even at chip operating temperatures of 85◦C. On the other hand, the devices have been observed to switch within a nanosecond. The switching speed measurements to date have been limited by parasitic capacitance and electrode resistance (RC time constant) and/or the instruments available rather than the device itself. The reading of the memristors is nondestructive and lowenergy. In order to interrogate the device state after switching, a lower voltage than the effective switching threshold is used without changing the device state. The ON/OFF conductance ratio of memristors is normally fairly large since a small position change of the mobile species results in a huge resistance change in these devices, which is usually between 1-1000, much larger than that of magnetic RAMs. Furthermore, the resistance state of the device is a continuous function of the position of the dopants and thus can be changed in an analog fashion. The large ON/OFF ratio and the analog behavior enable multilevel cell operation. Since the stack structure of the devices is as simple as a conductor/insulator/conductor tri-layer and all the layers can be polycrystalline or even amorphous, the layers can thus be stacked up indefinitely in principle. In addition, the fabrication processes and the materials used in the devices are all COMS compatible. Therefore, these devices are expected to have a low cost as well as a great manufacturability.
- Despite of the above mentioned promises, there are also some challenges for these devices to overcome before commercialization, including switching endurance, device yield, operating energy, electroforming, device nonlinearity and some issues related to integration. Switching endurance is how many cycles the device can be turned ON and OFF reversibly before the device performance degrades below some predefined level. The endurance values reported in the literature have been low, normally ranging from ten to a million cycles, which is not enough for a universal memory or a logic application. However, it seems that the endurance limit of metal-oxide memristors is still far from being reached. The key for a high endurance is to choose the right switching materials. T iOx− and T aOx−based memristors were fabricated with a similar device structure for comparison and a significantly better endurance was observed in the latter. Over 1012 switching cycles have recently been achieved in Ta oxide based devices [20]. The device yield has historically been low and needs to be significantly improved before it can be used in a commercial product. The control of the switching channels inside the devices plays a key role in determining the switchable device yield. One effective approach to control the formation of switching channels has been developed by seeding nanoscale switching centers [44]. Engineering the nanoscale ‘seeding’ improved the switchable device yield15 from below 30% to almost 100%. Partially due to the ultra-fast switching speed, the energy required to switch a device is small compared with other non-volatile memory technologies, such as Phase-change memory (> 10 PJ/bit). With an ever-increasing density, it is always better to reduce the operation power per bit for the devices. The switching current scales with the junction size and varies significantly with the switching materials, and thus reducing the device size and optimize the materials offers the best opportunity for minimizing switching energy. The T aOx based nanodevices (50 nm × 50 nm) can switch in under 2ns at less than 2V and less than 100μA, resulting in a sub pJ/bit operation energy. The ‘electroforming’ process is a one-time application of high voltage or current that produces a significant reduction of the electronic conductivity [41]. Subsequent to this change, the devices start to operate as tunable resistance switches, but with a wide variance of properties dependent on the details of the electroforming. Technologically, it is essential to eliminate this device variance for computer circuit applications. One approach to engineer a device that obviates electroforming is to thin the stoichiometric oxide film and add a thick sub-oxide layer, eliminating the ‘bulk’ region and just keeping the ‘switching interface’. In a crossbar structure, a so called access device is normally integrated at each crosspoint to select the target device for electrical operations, such as writing or reading. The purpose of using the access device is to limit the sneak path current, which is illustrated in Figure 13. For example, in order to write the device in green in Figure 13, − 1/2 Vwr is applied on the top electrode while + 1/2 Vwr is applied on the bottom electrode, where Vwr is the voltage that can switch the device. There is a voltage drop of 1/2 Vwr on all the other devices sharing either the top or bottom electrode with this target device and therefore currents flow through these devices, resulting in a ‘sneak path’ current, illustrated as a dashed purple line in Figure 13. The existence of the sneak path current causes many problems in crossbar circuits, including limiting the size of the crossbar, increasing power consumption, obscuring the reading sensitivity and so on. Sneak paths can be blocked with an access device such as a diode or transistor. Including access devices, however, increases the complexity and decreases the scalability of the crossbar structure. One of the major advantages of memristors used in a crossbar structure is the possibility to eliminate the need for access devices and still keep the sneak path current very low. The idea is illustrated in Figure 13 (b) and (c). The requirement for the memristors is a highly nonlinear I-V relation for the ON state (see Figure 13 (b)), while the OFF state is usually not an issue because the current level for the OFF state is low. This can be more clearly seen in the log-log scale plot in Figure 13 (c), where the subscripts wr and r represent writing and reading, respectively. With the large nonlinearity, the current level is very low at half of the writing voltage (1/2 Vwr) as well as the half of the reading voltage (1/2 Vr) for both the ON and OFF states, leading to a negligible sneak path current. The nonlinearity of the writing operation can be defined as the ratio between Iwr (the current level at Vwr) and I_1/2 wr (the current level at 1/2 Vwr). A similar definition can be made for the reading operation. According to this definition, the nonlinearity for a device with a linear I-V curve is 2. Most of the reported devices in the literature have a nonlinearity lower than 10, and a significantly improvement in nonlinearity is needed before these devices can be used in a large scale passive crossbar array without any access devices. As shown above, solutions exist in principle to effectively tackle each of the different challenges enumerated. However, a universal solution is needed to solve all the problems simultaneously, which at this writing is still missing. A better understanding of the nanoscopic structure and working mechanism for these memristors should lead to the desired universal solution.
- In conclusion, emerging non-volatile memory (NVM) technologies are bringing new opportunities as well as new challenges to the memory system design. It is important for us to understand and answer the challenges to enjoy the benefits of NVMs.
