## 📝 Introduction
### From: [[../../raw/note/yanDecipheringStabilityTwodimensional2025|Yan et al. 2025]]
- Two-dimensionalization unlocks the incredible potential of materials to exhibit unique and superior physical properties.
- Extending two-dimensionalization to non-layered crystalline materials presents both challenges and excitement.
- Fortunately, these studies have provided us with a substantial amount of data that can be used to decipher the underlying patterns or rules.
- We unveil a universal rule for creating stable two-dimensional counterparts of traditional high-performance semiconductors.
- Pave the way for expanding non-layered material systems into two-dimensional materials.

### From: [[../../raw/note/neumayerCompetingPolarPhases2025|Neumayer et al. 2025]]
- Feature a van der Waals gap where more than one polar phase can exist energetically close to the ground state.
- Offer unprecedented control over polar material properties through external stimuli.
- Overcome important size limitations and integration problems.
- Material platforms with tunable polar properties for multi-functional devices and sensors.
- Give an overview of future challenges and opportunities in this emerging field.

### From: [[../../raw/note/zhongHighthroughputExfoliationMultiferroic2025|Zhong et al. 2025]]
- A comprehensive high-throughput exfoliation strategy for non-vdW materials remains unestablished.
- Isolating an ABO3 monolayer necessarily begins with choosing the exfoliable crystallographic plane from its parent bulk.
- These materials not only harbor diverse multiferroic orders, but also possess intriguing physical properties.
- Our study builds an approach for the design and discovery of 2D non-vdW monolayers from crystallographic planes.

## 📝 Methods
### From: [[../../raw/note/yanDecipheringStabilityTwodimensional2025|Yan et al. 2025]]
- We employ high-throughput DFT calculation and machine learning to unveil a universal rule.
- Building blocks originate from the orbital hybridization and electron transfers, adhering to the Electron Counting Rule.
- Regression analysis reveals the energies as a linear superposition of the energies of building blocks.
- Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN) algorithm was used for classification.

### From: [[../../raw/note/neumayerCompetingPolarPhases2025|Neumayer et al. 2025]]
- Highlight the co-existence of polar properties found on local scales using advanced local characterization techniques.
- Methods to control polar properties in transition metal thio-/selenophosphates.
- Direct structural verification of in-gap ferroelectric and antiferroelectric phases.

### From: [[../../raw/note/zhongHighthroughputExfoliationMultiferroic2025|Zhong et al. 2025]]
- We propose a bond density and binding strength criterion to identify exfoliable crystallographic planes.
- We screened 35 exfoliable and stable ABO3 monolayers using a systematic investigation.

## 📝 Results & Discussion
### From: [[../../raw/note/yanDecipheringStabilityTwodimensional2025|Yan et al. 2025]]
- Akin to assembling with LEGO, various building blocks are arranged in different configurations.
- These structures demonstrated superior energetic stability compared to theoretically predicted counterparts.
- The tetrahedron building blocks contribute the most to stabilization, followed by triangular ones.
- The enhanced stability significantly reduces phonon scattering of carriers, leading to exceptional electronic properties.
- The crystal has a rectangular symmetry.
- The coefficients of building block proportion and the fitting quality parameter R-squared value are shown.

### From: [[../../raw/note/neumayerCompetingPolarPhases2025|Neumayer et al. 2025]]
- Polar phases can be transformed into each other through external stimuli.
- Energetically close polar phases offer a tunable material platform.
- The co-existence of polar properties highlights the richness of the phase diagram.

### From: [[../../raw/note/zhongHighthroughputExfoliationMultiferroic2025|Zhong et al. 2025]]
- The giant spin splitting arises primarily from the system’s symmetries and p−d orbital interactions.
- Symmetry-driven structural switching affords a direct, reversible route between semiconducting and half-metallic states.
- The phase transition barrier energies are as low as 9.1 meV/atom.
- Offering versatile phase-controlled electronic and spin capabilities.
- The combination of a large J1 and a high number of spin neighbors significantly enhances the system’s magnetic ordering.
